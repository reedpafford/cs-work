## The shape
```
### B-roll
- "forty feet from a dorm with two hundred girls in it" → wide shot, dorm at night, lit windows
- "she pulled the pin" → close-up, pin pulled, keychain swinging
- product block → alarm on a keyring, a hand clipping it to a backpack
```
One bullet per cue: the exact spoken words it lands on, in quotes (or the beat name for a whole block), an arrow, then the shot. The script section is never touched; B-roll lives only in its own section, so the script stays clean for the voice.

## What gets a cue
1. **The line she has to picture to keep watching.** Principle 2's specifics (the number, the place, the object) are where a shot pays; abstract lines don't get one.
2. **The mechanism.** The alarm, the pull, the sound, the people turning: always shown, always the real hardware (a keychain alarm, a pin, 130 dB). A viewer believes what she sees over what she's told.
3. **The product block and the offer.** The alarm in a hand, on a keyring, on a backpack; the bundle (three plus two free) as a physical pile, never a price graphic with `$` or `%`.
4. **Not every line.** The talking head carries most of the video; B-roll is where it raises the stakes or proves the claim. Roughly one cue per two or three beats, denser in the story, lighter in the CTA.

## What never goes on screen (principle 8, the image is the lever)
- Nothing that shows or suggests a sexual threat: no girl pinned, no hand over a mouth in close-up, no drink being spiked, no one being dragged. Show the before (the empty parking lot, the open car door, the phone at 3% battery) and the after (the police lights, the mother's face, the empty bed).
- No weapons in frame, no before/after, no worded guarantee on screen ("stops," "saves").
- A competing solution on screen (pepper spray, Life360) makes it SA (hooks.md); only when the script names it.
- The banned words are banned here too; the scanner checks this section.
