# RUNBOOK — Podcast (a guest and a host, a clip from a longer talk)
> Trigger: "podcast" · "podcast style" · `Format: Podcast`. **DRAFT (2026-11-19)**, written from RP21, the first podcast brief, which read like a tips list instead of the brand's ads. Reed's edits on the next podcast brief rewrite this file (principle 9).

## What it is
A clip that looks cut from a longer conversation: a guest with authority (an investigator, a detective, a nurse, a founder) and a host who stands in for the viewer. The format buys credibility (nobody's selling, they're just talking) and lets the authority deliver the pitch as a recommendation (`craft/playbook.md` §7). The script file uses the movie shape for speakers: `Name: line`, with a `Cast:` line in the header.

## Steal the swipe's content, then funnel (Reed, RP21, 2026-11-19)
The case-led rewrite (a named girl, the signs coming out of her story) was "bad bad." Reed's call: "write closer to the tiktok, basically stealing its content, just funnel it to buying the product." So when the swipe is an informational clip that already works (a "4 signs" countdown):
- **Keep its list, its order and its wording, near-verbatim.** Change only what routes it to the buyer (her daughter instead of "you") and what the word rule needs.
- **Keep the host's short reactions where the swipe has them** ("Oh my gosh." "No." "That's scary."), then give the host one real question at the end of the list, the one the mom watching is thinking ("What if she doesn't see any of it?"), and that question is the turn.
- **Don't invent a story the swipe doesn't have.** The value is the list; the ad's only addition is the funnel after it.

## The two voices
- **The guest** gets a persona register like any authority (`brand.md`): what they've seen, said flat, with their own words, never the stock openers ("Look," "Here's the thing") by default.
- **The host** is the viewer's mouth, not a reaction track. One-word reactions ("No." "Oh my gosh.") are seasoning, once or twice; the host's real job is the question the viewer is thinking ("What if she doesn't see any of it?"), which becomes the turn into the product.

**From Reed's RP21 pass:** the guest gives orders, not descriptions ("Don't touch it. She needs to get in her car and get to safety."). The host's lines are recognition, not just reactions: "I think I've heard this one" on a sign, and "Oh wait, I've heard of these! How do we get one?" on the brand, so the host is social proof for the product as well as the viewer's mouth.

## The back half
The guest carries the sell as a recommendation ("That's why we've been telling moms…"), then the brand's product block, offer, share CTA and close from `write.md`, in the guest's voice. The host can ask where to get one, and can say she's heard of it; the host never pitches. The guest points at the link ("It'll be linked to this post") before the sale, and "not affiliated" comes as an afterthought ("Oh and I'm not affiliated with them at all").

## The interview variant (RP25, 2026-11-24, DRAFT)
A reporter and the threat himself (an abductor, face blurred, dark room; the CNN cartel-interview shape). The hook is the reporter's question and his answer is the bridge. The reporter stays neutral and asks in whole, natural sentences ("Do you have any idea how many girls it was?"), never one-word prompts. He's emotionless but not robotic (Reed): a chuckle, a small joke, "I'm not sure," "we," a crew around him. The product never comes from him; it's his grudge ("the ones I really hated were the girls with those little alarms"), and the reporter closes by reporting what she found and telling the viewer to do the same.
