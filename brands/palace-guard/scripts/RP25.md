Problem-aware, not solution-aware. · What: an interview with a man who was paid to abduct girls: how common it was, how easy, the nice places he picked, what he can't tell us, what made him leave, how he got caught; then the reporter tells the viewer she found the alarm that works and urges her to do the same. · How: Reed's three hooks as the interviewer's questions, his answer as each bridge, a chain of neutral questions carrying A to D, the reporter's close with RP20b's product block in her voice.
Avatar: mothers and grandmothers 45 to 70 of a daughter 16 to 25 who drives herself · Sub-avatar: she knows abductions happen, just not in her town.
Desire: her daughter is the girl he'd skip.
Angle: they work the nice areas because the girls there have their guard down, and the moves that make the news are the ones they've already quit, so knowing them doesn't keep her safe.
Mechanism: he can't have anyone looking at him; one pull and the whole lot looks, so he leaves, because there's always another girl.
Authority: the man himself; the reporter reports what she found.
Route: Interview (new): hook question → his answer → A how common → B close to home → easy → the markers they quit → what they do now, withheld → C what stopped him → caught → D the news is behind → the reporter: D again, the alarm, product, offer, not affiliated, regret, share, link and "Don't wait."
