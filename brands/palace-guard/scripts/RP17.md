# RP17 — You worry too much (the movie)
Brand: palace-guard · Persona: mother (Lisa, lead and close) · Awareness: PA · Format: Movie
Status: drafting
Parent: — · Lever: —
Cast: Lisa (the mom, early 50s, the one who worries) · Mark (the dad, mid 50s, easygoing, sure nothing can happen to his girl) · Emily (19, a sophomore two hours from home) · Jess (Emily's roommate, seen only leaving) · Connor (a junior from her stats class, friendly, the guy everybody likes) · the RA · the campus police officer (50s, flat and quiet) · the news anchor (on the TV)

## Memo
Why: the buyer who already has this fear has been told she's overreacting, by her husband and by her daughter; no movie of ours has put that on screen, and RP13 named the beat we haven't dramatized ("walked home by somebody she met an hour ago"). · What: on the night of a party, intercut, the dad tells the mom she worries too much and the daughter tells her to stop texting, while a nice boy from her class walks her home and won't take no at her dorm door, and the alarm the dad called twenty bucks of paranoia is what stops it. · How: RP10's movie shape (the hooks first, then the body, the expert after), the dramatic-irony intercut from movie.md (the dot, the oven), RP13's walked-home beat.
Avatar: mothers 45 to 65 of a daughter in college · Sub-avatar: the mother who already lies awake over it, whose husband says she watches too much news and whose daughter says "please stop."
Desire: her daughter coming home the same girl she left, and being able to sleep.
Angle: she's watching her own fear play out; the danger isn't a stranger, it's the guy from class who offers to walk her home, and the mom who worries isn't crazy, she's the only one paying attention (Reed: a grounded assault, not an abduction).
Mechanism: unwanted attention; he's counting on just the two of them behind a door, her word against his, and one pull opens every door on the hall. The tracking apps she's mocked for (Life360, Find My) show her the dot at the dorm and let her go to sleep; they tell her where her daughter is, never who she's with.
Authority: the campus police officer in the dorm lounge that night.
Route: Movie (craft/formats/movie.md); the body starts the night of it, no flashbacks, the parents and the daughter intercut, Lisa's V.O. close.

## Body
### Script

[SCENE 1 — campus, the walk back from a party, a little after midnight]
(Emily and Connor cut across the dark quad, past the library, the lights off; music from a party fades behind them)
(she's a little tipsy, her shoes in one hand, her keys hanging off a finger, a pink palace guard on the ring, subtly)
(her phone buzzes: Mom: Are you back at the dorm yet?)
(she rolls her eyes and types: Mom. I'm fine. Please stop.)
Connor: Jess just ditched you, by the way.
Emily (laughing): Jess always ditches me.
Connor: Is she coming back tonight?
Emily: No, she's gone all weekend. She basically lives at her boyfriend's.
Connor: So you've got the room to yourself.
(she laughs, and doesn't answer)
(his arm slides around her waist)
Connor: You know you're really cute when you're drunk.
(she laughs again, smaller this time, and steps out from under his arm)
Emily: Okay. It's right up here.

[SCENE 2 — the side door of Emily's dorm]
(Emily swipes her card; the lock clicks green)
Emily: Well, this is me. Thank you, seriously.
Connor: Let me come up. Just for a little.
Emily: I'm really tired, Connor.
Connor: I saw how you were looking at me. Don't act like you don't want this.
Emily: Connor, no, seriously. I'm going up by myself.

[SCENE 3 — the kitchen, the same minute]
(the news still on the TV)
(Mark rinses his cup in the sink; Lisa sits at the table with her phone face up in front of her)
Mark: You know what your problem is? You watch too much of that stuff.
Lisa: It's the news, Mark, calm down! Maybe you should care more about the world we just sent our daughter into!
Mark: It's the worst thing that happened in the state, daily. All it does is freak you out. That stuff doesn't happen to people like us!
(Mark grabs her phone so he can see it: Mom. I'm fine. Please stop.)
Mark: And what did I tell you about this? She doesn't need you checking on her every night.
Mark: I mean seriously, Lisa. You have Life360, Find My Friends, and you text her asking to check in when she gets home from parties.
(Lisa looks ashamed, but she still believes she's doing the right thing)
Mark: She's in college. A nice college, and she's out with her friends. Leave her alone.
(he tosses her phone on the couch, still going)
(Lisa picks her phone up off the couch; nothing new)
Lisa: I just want to know she's safe.
Mark: If something was wrong, we'd know! I can't believe she puts up with this!
(she turns off the TV; the kitchen goes dark)

[SCENE 4 — the dorm door]
(she pulls the door open; he puts his hand flat on it, above her head)
Emily: Connor, I'm going to bed.
(his hand closes around her wrist)
(he shoves the door wider and starts to push his way inside with her; the hallway behind the glass is empty)
Connor: Don't make this weird, Em. Just be quiet.
(she is trying to pull away, obviously not wanting to be there, squirming)
(the keys are still on her finger)

[SCENE 5 — Lisa and Mark's bedroom]
(Mark switches off the lamp on his side)
(Lisa opens Life360 one more time: Emily's dot sits right on her dorm)
(she lets out a breath, puts the phone face down, and closes her eyes)

[SCENE 6 — the front of the dorm]
(in the doorway, Emily gets two fingers through the pin)
(she pulls)
(the siren rings off the brick; the strobe flashes white on Connor's face)
(he lets go of her wrist)
(lights snap on in the windows, one floor after another; a window slides open above them)
RA (O.S.): Hey! What's going on down there?
(an RA in pajama pants comes through the front doors, three girls behind her)
(Connor backs off the steps and runs across the lawn)
(Emily slides down the brick wall by the door, the alarm still screaming in her hand)

[SCENE 7 — Lisa and Mark's bedroom]
(Mark asleep; Lisa asleep, her phone face down on the nightstand)
(the phone lights up and buzzes against the wood: Emily)
Lisa: Em?
Emily (on the phone, sobbing): Mom. (sobbing) I don't know what happened… (rambles)
(Lisa sits straight up)
Lisa: What happened? Emily, where are you?
Emily (on the phone): He wouldn't let go of me. He was going to.. (rambles, crying) I pulled the alarm.
(Mark sits up and sees her face)
(he shows regret, then worry, like he feels bad for telling her she was crazy earlier; he's already pulling on his jeans)
(cut to black)

[SCENE 8 — the dorm lounge, a little after two]
(Emily on a couch in a hoodie, the RA next to her)
(the pink alarm on the coffee table, the pin pushed back in)
(the campus police officer in a chair across from her, a notepad on his knee)
(Lisa and Mark come through the door; Lisa doesn't stop until she's holding her)
Officer: You're the parents? I'm sorry to meet like this. At least she's okay.
Mark: What happened? Who was it?
Emily: Connor. He sits two rows behind me in stats. I let him walk me home.
Mark: A kid from her class? Come on. He probably just had too much to drink.
Officer: Sir, I take a call like this almost every weekend.
Officer: One in five girls on a campus like this gets assaulted before she graduates. And almost every time, it's somebody she knows.
Lisa: One in five?
Officer: And those are just the ones who report it. Most don't, because it's her word against his, and they'd both been drinking.
Officer: Look, it goes the same way almost every time. He waits until her friends leave, he offers to walk her home, and if the coast is clear… you know the rest.
Officer: So think about what was standing between her and that door tonight.
Officer: Nobody was coming. The only reason anybody came is that thing on her keys.
Officer: One pull, and half that building was at their windows. That's the one thing a guy like him can't have. Attention.
Officer: Without that alarm, we'd be having a very different conversation tonight.
Officer: I've actually seen this one all around campus. The Palace Guard, right? Who got it for her?
(Mark looks at Lisa)
Officer: Good. I've pulled those cheap ones out of evidence bags. Either a dead battery, or it broke the first time it hit the ground. Not these. 
Officer (to Lisa): You did a great job keeping her safe.
Officer (to Emily): And you were incredibly brave.
(the officer steps out into the hall with the RA)
(Mark picks the alarm up off the coffee table and turns it over in his hand)
Mark: I said that stuff doesn't happen to people like us.
(Lisa doesn't say anything)
Mark: I'm sorry, Lisa. You were right.
(Emily reaches over and takes her mom's hand)
Emily: You can text me as much as you want, Mom.
(Lisa laughs, and it turns into crying)

[SCENE 9 — the close, a few weeks later, back on campus: Lisa's V.O. and the CTA]
(Emily walks across the quad to class in the sun with two friends, laughing, her keys swinging off her finger, the pink alarm on the ring)
Lisa (V.O.): I'm the one in my house who worries. I've got Life360 on her phone, I text her after every party, and I know it drives her crazy.
Lisa (V.O.): My husband told me I watch too much news. My daughter told me to stop.
(Emily in the library, her keys on the table next to her laptop)
Lisa (V.O.): That night I watched her dot get to her dorm, and I finally put my phone down. She was at the door with a boy from her class. The nice one. The one I never would have worried about.
Lisa (V.O.): My phone could tell me where she was. It couldn't do a thing about who she was with.
Lisa (V.O.): But I'd put an alarm on her keys, and when she couldn't get away, it screamed for her.
(night; Emily walks back from a party with her roommate, arm in arm, the alarm in her hand)
Lisa (V.O.): She still goes out with her friends. She still walks home at night. She's not scared, because she knows that thing is on her keys.
Lisa (V.O.): So if you're the one who worries, you're not crazy. You're just the only one paying attention.
(the alarm in a hand: the pin, the strobe)
Lisa (V.O.): It's called Palace Guard. It's louder than an ambulance siren, it's got a strobe light so nobody can pretend they didn't see, and it's lighter than her car key. It's even got an anti-misfire feature, so it won't go off in her bag in the middle of class.
Lisa (V.O.): I bought three and got two free, so it was less than twenty dollars each. One for Emily, one for her sister, one for my mom, and two for her roommates.
Lisa (V.O.): A detective gal makes them in small batches, and they keep selling out.
(Emily swipes into her dorm, alone, and the door shuts behind her; she texts: Home. Love you.)
(Lisa in bed, reads it, smiles, puts the phone face down, and goes to sleep)
Lisa (V.O.): I still text her. But I sleep now.
Lisa (V.O.): Send this to every mom who's been told she worries too much. And send it to her husband, too.
Lisa (V.O.): The link's right there. Please, don't wait until it's too late.
