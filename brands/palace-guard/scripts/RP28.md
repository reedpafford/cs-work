## Memo
Why: RP17 sold the save; nothing of ours has sold the regret of not buying. The mother watching our ads is scrolling past them, so this movie shows her doing exactly that, the same night it happens. · What: the last night before winter break, Kate is assaulted behind her dorm door while her mom, two hours away, scrolls past an ad for an alarm like she always does; Kate comes home for the holidays changed, her dad calls her moody, her mom finds out, and the rest is the mom's guilt in voice-over, imagining the version where she bought it, which the viewer sees. · How: RP17's shape (the mom leads, the dad dismisses, the V.O. close), four hooks that test the level of the approach (the nice guy, a stranger, her friend, her ex), all grounded, all ending behind the same door, then a back half that's mostly Linda's internal V.O. over what is cut against what would have been.
Avatar: mothers 45 to 65 of a daughter in college · Sub-avatar: the mom who has seen these ads and scrolled past, because she didn't have a reason.
Desire: never having to live with "what if."
Angle: she's scrolled past these ads a hundred times because they were for other people's daughters; the belief shift is that it can happen to yours. Kate doesn't get her life back, and the mom's warning to the viewer is don't make the same assumption.
Mechanism: attention; one pull at the door and the whole building knows. Shown in the would-have, never explained.
Authority: none; the would-have sequence is the proof.
Route: Movie (craft/formats/movie.md), no time jumps, no expert; Linda's internal V.O. runs from the moment she knows to the end and turns to the viewer for the close.
