## Why this lever exists
A language change reaches a different sub-audience on the same body, so it does not cannibalize the English winner. It is the cheapest new-audience pull we have: proven script, proven persona, one dimension changed. Pull it on winners only (a running RP with a Rank), not on unproven scripts.

## The rule
**Clean, simple, native Spanish. No American slang or phrasing translated word for word.** A Spanish script is not English with Spanish words on it. If the narrator is a Latina mother or a detective talking to Latino parents, they talk like it: native idiom, native rhythm, no U.S. figures of speech. Decorative Spanish on an English spine reads as foreign to the target and is worse than either language clean.

## The unit
One English winner → one full-Spanish brief. Same script, same persona, same hook engine, same product block, same three hook variations translated. No Spanglish variation, no language ladder. If a Spanish audience needs a different hook or beat, that is a different brief, not a translation.

## How to translate (in this order)
1. **Architecture stays locked.** Same beats, same order, same hook engine, same product block, same close. Section count matches the English. Nothing added, nothing cut.
2. **Translate the meaning, not the words.** Each line asks: what does this line make the mother feel, and how would a Mexican mother say that to her comadre? Then write that sentence. A literal translation of a good English line is a bad Spanish line.
3. **First principles still apply, in Spanish.** Simpler, visual, mannerism, sixth-grade language, one way to read the line. Connected speech; a fragment lands, never leads. The persona's register holds: the detective is still plain and steady, the mother is still unpolished.
4. **Numbers, prices, units spelled out in Spanish** the way the mouth says them: *ciento treinta decibeles*, *menos de veinte dólares cada una*, *treinta días*. The voice reads exactly what is written.
5. **Pick one address register and hold it.** *Mija* / *mi niña* (mother to daughter, warm) ≠ *señora* (detective to the parent, respectful) ≠ *usted* vs *tú*. A detective addresses the parent as *usted*. A mother telling her story to other mothers uses *tú* or speaks to *ustedes*. Never mix mid-script.
6. **Kill every U.S. idiom.** "Red zone" needs a native rendering, not a calque; "every penny back" → *te devuelven hasta el último centavo*; "grab one" → *agarra una*; "junk" → *lo corriente*; "the cheap ones off Amazon" → *las baratas de Amazon*. Any pop-culture reference (Dora the Explorer survives, she is Latina; a U.S. TV cop show does not).
7. **Family and faith words carry the emotional load** in this market. *Nietas*, *mija*, *que Dios la cuide*, *la familia* land harder than any mechanism line. Put them where the English had its emotional beats, not everywhere.
8. **Run the scanner** on the Spanish draft. Digits, caps, fragment runs, and repeated shapes are language-neutral. The hook blocks unscanned writes in Spanish too.
9. **Native gut-check before it ships.** My Spanish is a draft. A native Mexican-American ear is the verdict. Flag every line I'm unsure of, in a list, in chat.
10. **Finalize per the finalize runbook.** Name token adds the language: `RP##_authority_parents_PA_Alarm_es_first_three_words`. Awareness read is unchanged. Text Hook: Reed writes it in Spanish; keyword caps work the same (*Tu HIJA en la UNIVERSIDAD...😳*).

Plates translate the same way, keyword caps kept: "3 PREDATORS almost ABDUCTED my DAUGHTER…😳" → **3 DEPREDADORES casi SECUESTRAN a mi HIJA…😳** · "How PREDATORS ABDUCT GIRLS at TARGET…🚨" → **Cómo SECUESTRAN los DEPREDADORES a MUCHACHAS en TARGET…🚨**. Bank additions: sweatsuit → **sudadera y pants**; the loudest one at dinner → **la que más habla en la mesa** (*gritona* can read as an insult); not a force field → **no hace milagros**; anti-misfire → **una función para que no se active sola** (*seguro* is the pin); the second location → **a otro lugar** (*segundo lugar* is second place in a race); feisty → **bravita**; assaulted → **agredida** (never *violación* or *abuso sexual*, the word rule).

## U.S. phrasing to kill on sight
"red zone" as *zona roja* (means red-light district in Mexico; render as *las primeras semanas, las más peligrosas*) · "safe choice" · "the whole plan" · "junk" · "evidence bags" (explain: *las que me han llegado como evidencia*) · "predator repellent" · "on the job" · any U.S. cop-show or campus-culture reference · "welcome week" (→ *la semana de bienvenida* only if the school calls it that; otherwise *los primeros días*).

## Targeting note
Mexican-first wording above. If Reed targets pan-U.S.-Hispanic (Caribbean, Central American), swap the Mexican-specific words (*jalar*, *bolsa*, *mija*) for neutral ones (*tirar/halar*, *cartera*, *mi hija*). Match the words to the audience the ad set actually reaches.

## Never
- Translate line by line and ship it. (JK151 audit: literal lines were the ones that failed.)
- Mix languages. No Spanglish, no Spanish flavor on an English script. It is one or the other.
- Change anything but the language. If the Spanish version needs a different hook, it's a different brief, not a translation.
- Ship without the native check.
