- **The buyer is the parent or grandparent. The daughter is the user.** Mothers, fathers, grandmothers. Every benefit routes back through the person paying.
- **Peace of mind is the product.** They're buying the end of the movie in their head at one a.m., and the proof that they did the thing a good parent does. Show the daughter safe, sell the parent calm.
- **Optimism bias is the gate** ("it won't happen to her"). Routing through the loved one helps kill it: a parent can't run denial on their daughter's nervous system. **How we break the bias is still being tested.** Don't treat any one method as proven.
- **The overarching engine is manufactured imminence.** The ad's job is to make them feel they have to buy it right now. The vehicle can be anything: a detective listicle, a mother's story, a real case, a red-zone clock. The imminence is the constant; the vehicle is the variable.
- **The setup sells one belief before the mechanism: attention is the best weapon.** If she draws a scene, it stops him, because these men need quiet, surprise, and nobody looking. **Pepper spray is never bashed (Reed, 2026-09-16, sharpened).** The two are too different to compare down. We praise it, then say the two true things: it's dangerous (it needs a free hand, aim, distance, no wind, and it gets taken and turned on her), and it's banned almost everywhere she actually goes (school, college, concerts, stadiums, the airport, the bus). Then we explain why the alarm is better entirely: no aim, no range, no law, can't be used against her, and it does the one thing that ends it, it gets her heard. The narrative in front of the mechanism is built to make that comparison land, not bolted on after. We say the real thing she needs first is attention. Only after that belief lands do we go to our mechanism.
- **Our mechanism is the freeze**, said two ways: we trained the scream out of her by twelve, and it's natural tonic immobility, an instinct every mammal has. The wording and where it sits in the narrative are still being tested. The section as a whole works in the account.
- **Plain beats clever in the hook (2026-09-14, CBO Sep 8–14).** The hook with the plainest shock words carries the account: RP19 V3 "My oldest was assaulted during her first week of college" ($998, 23 purchases, ROAS 1.70). Rules: `hooks.md`, Comprehension first.
- **Positioning: everything Birdie is not (Reed, 2026-09-14).** She's Birdie is the name people know (Birdie 3.0, pull-pin, 130 dB, strobe, $34.95, "Made for Women, by Women," "4,000,000 women"). Their hardware is ours, so the alarm itself is never the differentiator. Three edges, in order: (1) we open unaware and problem-aware (the freeze, attention, the red zone); they sell product-first to the woman. (2) we talk to the parent and grandparent buying for her; they talk to her. (3) they have a documented subscription wound (Birdie+ app, trial counted from purchase so gifts expire in transit, cancellation complaints on Trustpilot and BBB); we are one purchase, no app, no monthly fee, nothing to cancel. Everything else that's ours and visible: anti-misfire feature (the #1 objection is false-alarm embarrassment), the flashlight, white and small, the balcony drop, tiny company, a former detective's pick. **Physical-product rule:** the mechanism has to match what's on screen (pin, siren, strobe, anti-misfire, flashlight, weight), so the invented room is in the psychology (freeze, attention, predator behavior) and the category (why the cheap ones end up in a drawer), never in the hardware. **The product is logo-less (Reed, RP28, 2026-09-16):** nobody reads the name off the device; a character who shopped for it says "Palace Guard" once, with the reason she knows it. **Legal line:** name Birdie only with what's on their own pages or in public complaints (the subscription, the trial timing, the cancellation problem); never claim they're quieter, break, or misfire without proof; "the pink one" or "the one everybody's heard of" is always safe.
- **This is a preventative market, not a painful one (Reed, 2026-09-16).** Nobody wakes up wanting a safety alarm; there is no daily pain, so there are almost no solution-aware buyers to catch. She buys when an ad makes the danger present and the fear is stronger than the price, the same way beetroot sold on the fear of a heart attack. So every ad opens unaware or problem-aware and installs one belief: her daughter could be assaulted at any moment, and the alarm is how she stops it. Story is the best installer, and the front half of an unaware story doesn't need the product at all (that's why the Rosabella winners transpose). SA and product-aware are bottom-of-funnel ads behind a winner, never the opener.
- **Sell it like it works.** Aggressive, confident claims, because the product does exactly this. The only reason we don't say a hundred percent is that no parent would believe it. Believability is the ceiling, not compliance.

- **A pasted line is a swipe's first line. Find the full ad before doing anything (Reed, 2026-10-17: "whenever I give you a line like that, look in the swipes database and find the full ad and transpose it").** Search the Swipes DS (`collection://3cfb1be2-c8a2-80d9-b5d7-000be201941b`, the `First Line` property) with `notion-search` on the line's distinctive words, fetch the page, read the whole ad and its `Style` (LFS → `LFS.md`, video → this section), then make the brief and transpose all of it. Never write hooks or a body from the line alone, and never ask whether there's more of the ad before searching. If nothing matches, say so and ask for the rest.
- **Keep the hook as close to verbatim as it will go, and keep most of the lead as the swipe had it.** The hook and lead are what it won on. Match shape and substance; write the few signature phrases ourselves so it isn't a word-for-word lift.
- **Spend the change budget on the back:** the turn (attention → freeze), the product block, the objection counters, the bundle, the close. All from section three and the most recent brief.
- **Route to the buyer at word one.** A swipe that talks to the woman herself gets re-pointed at the parent in the first three words. "Why women can't scream" → "Why your daughter can't scream."
- **Segment the subject away from the viewer.** "Women" → "girls her age." The parent must never read the line as about herself.
- **Placeholders over guesses.** Anything that needs Reed's call goes in parentheses; only the obvious gets swapped and bolded.
- **A real near-miss story (Reddit and the like, Reed, RP30 and RP31).** Retell it in our narrator's words, nothing verbatim, change the names and details. Keep what made it true: the small talk, the matching sweatsuits, the "only kidding," the looking over his shoulder. Put the alarm at the one point where the story can only go two ways (his hand on the car door; the van door sliding open), with every other safeguard present in that second and no use (her voice, her strength, her keys, the store, the tracking dot). No contact: the men never touch her; the noise ends it (Reed, RP31). Cut the source's padding (the self-defense inventory, the side characters) and anything the alarm makes redundant (the escort).
- **What makes a near-miss story stick (from the seven Reddit swipes, 2026-09-18):** the helper was the predator (the pediatrician lot: the man under the hood wasn't the mechanic) · an object that proves what was about to happen without saying it (freshly sharpened shears on top of the engine) · the lure asked three times, each more irritated ("come up here really quick, I'll show you") · politeness as the trap, so the reader screams at her · the first bad sign explained away · engineered isolation (the car that won't start, the last appointment, the empty lot) · the reveal after, from someone who sees what she didn't (the second mechanic, the CCTV "fifty feet behind me," the husband) · a witness showing up is what saves her in every one, which is exactly what the alarm manufactures on demand · the scale-up at the end (other women missing, other attempts across the street) · honest uncertainty and a self-correction ("it was a Challenger, not a Charger") as the believability. Raise stakes by making it premeditated (he chose her, he disabled the car, he'd done it before), making the trusted person the fake, and confirming the ending through someone official.
- **The vehicle is something nobody looks at twice (Reed, RP58, 2026-10-18).** A white van with no windows has been in RP31, RP40 and RP44, and viewers now read it as a cliché. A big black Suburban with the back seats taken out is "less sus and more hidden": a family car on the outside, with the empty back seen through the open door as the proof. On RP59 Reed wrote "a sketchy van parked there all alone" at the dark end of the lot, because there the van is something she is being walked toward, so it reads as the second location. The point is a vehicle a bystander wouldn't look at twice, not "never a van."
- **Ask which job you're doing before writing a word:** a swipe already aimed at our buyer keeps its front; a far transposition rewrites the body from our market.

## 5. Structure
- **What the three parts mean (Reed, 2026-09-19).** The **lead** is the whole first story of the video, not the sentence after the hook: hook, retention beat, then the story runs until it ends. The **body** is the mechanism, the product differentiation and the common beats. The **CTA** is the end, and it's the same every time. So "tighten the lead" means the story, and the beat after the hook is the retention beat.
- **Hooks and leads:** the structure changes with the vehicle. Story, listicle, insider binary, real case. See `hooks.md`.
- **Retention beat, right after the authority line (Reed, RP20, 2026-09-14):** promise the fix as "one thing" without naming it, then say what comes first. "This could've been easily prevented if she just had this one thing. First, I'll explain what actually happened."
- **When the front is a story, the back half carries only what the story left unmoved (Reed, RP28 then RP30; twice, so it's a step, not a note).** Before writing a word after the turn, list every belief the scene has already shifted by showing it (she won't scream, it's loud, anyone can pull it, he leaves, the pin goes back in, she'd be too embarrassed). None of those gets a line in the back half. What's left is usually four things: what was really happening, why not the cheap ones, the name, and what she bought. The body-and-CTA order below is the inventory for a lecture vehicle (RP14, RP18); in a story vehicle each of those beats is either already shown or it arrives as something that happened to the narrator (the cheap ones in her cart and her husband saying don't; "it rode around on my purse strap for a month and never went off once"), never as a list of facts. The authority can be borrowed (a husband who grew up with a deputy for a father) and doesn't need to arrive.
- **Teach the mechanism on the girl who lost, not the girl who was saved (Reed, RP33, 2026-09-20).** The case victim did everything right and fought back, and it didn't matter because nobody heard her, so the mechanism is proven before the product exists and before the narrator's own daughter shows up. The daughter's rescue then reads as proof of a rule the viewer already believes, instead of as the claim itself. Order: the case teaches → the family story proves → the product → the CTA.
- **Compliment the buyer, then move the threat (Reed, RP33).** "I'm proud of parents these days, our daughters are getting much better at defending themselves" before "but predators have their online communities too, and they're getting smarter." The buyer's effort is respected, so the fear lands on something she hasn't already handled, and the detective's insider access ("I've read their chats") is the one claim only this persona owns.
- **Name the denial and pin it on the mother who had it (Reed, RP33):** "Every parent watching this is sure it won't be their daughter. So was Ms. Doyle."
- **The misfire answer comes before the junk-alarm beat (Reed, RP34, 2026-09-21).** Order in the CTA: guardrail → "the first thing Ellie asked me was whether it would go off in her backpack" → junk alarms → "I don't cheap out" → the name → specs. Her real objection is answered before the category is separated.
- **In a reported block, "He said" opens it and then the lines run bare, and the block ends on the story's proof, not the tagline (Reed, RP38, 2026-09-22).** He took "He said" off three of my five officer lines and moved the mechanism lines above "she handed him a security guard and a nurse in about ten seconds." The tagline ("Noise is predator repellent") sits inside the block; the last line is what happened.
- **Never bash the spray; concede it, then move past it (Reed, RP36 "Those can help," RP40 "Yes they're still useful," 2026-09-29; twice, so it's a rule).** The buyer bought the spray. Calling it useless calls her a fool; saying it's still useful and "won't always work" keeps her with you and opens the gap the alarm fills.
- **The retention beat is one short sentence, no plea (Reed, RP51 and RP52, 2026-10-16; twice the same day, a rule).** The stake and nothing else: "If you have a daughter, this video could save her life." · "Moms, this could save your girl from being traumatized for life." No "please watch to the end," no "please don't scroll," no second audience tacked on ("or a granddaughter"). **On RP58 Reed cut it entirely**, opening the body on the story ("My daughter Olivia is seventeen"). That's one cut, not yet a rule, so RP59 was drafted without it to test.
- **The retention promise can include the mother's own life (Reed, RP36 "heck, even your own life," RP40 "heck, even yours"; twice, a rule).** One clause widens the stake without changing the cohort.
- **The proof as "why do you think" questions (Reed, RP36 "Why do you think he disabled my car, and covered my mouth first?", RP40 "Why do you think the van has no windows? Why do you think his hand was over Hailey's mouth?"; twice, a rule).** The mechanism isn't explained, it's asked: point at the details the viewer already saw, and let her answer "so nobody sees" herself. Then one line states it ("If one person gets a glimpse of his face or his plate, we can catch him within the hour"). **Only when the scene hasn't already shown it (Reed, RP58, 2026-10-18: cut both questions, "they didn't seem necessary").** When the story itself shows him needing her out of the car and away from eyes, the questions restate it and come out.
- **The share CTA can end on the audience (Reed, RP59 and RP60, 2026-10-22, twice, so a rule):** "Share this with every mom who has a daughter going out this Halloween." · "Share this with every mom whose kids are going trick-or-treating this Saturday." He cut the "so their daughter doesn't get abducted or assaulted" tail both times when the audience clause already carries the stakes. Default now: name the audience and stop; add the tail only when the audience is generic ("every mother you know").
- **The share CTA, on every script (Reed, RP40, 2026-09-29; "always add the share CTA there, that's part of our WM"):** before the link line, so the close runs share → link and scarcity → "Don't wait" (order set on the big-three pass): "Share this with every mother you know so their daughter doesn't get abducted or assaulted." The buyer becomes the distributor, and the worst words stay in the spoken line (never the Primary Text).
- **No setup line before the success story (Reed, RP40).** He cut my "Now let me tell you about a girl who made it home." The next story starts on its time stamp ("Six weeks after Hailey…"); announcing it is narrator overhead.
- **The detective belief chain, RP41 order (Reed, 2026-09-29):** outrage said out loud ("An essay, are you kidding me. They should be locked up for life.") → the pivot to before ("nobody's asking what could have stopped the rape before it happened, and I'm not talking about putting up a fight") → the fight and the spray killed in one line each ("Pepper spray is banned on most campuses, and I haven't seen one girl beat up 7 men") → "she can outsmart them" → the weakness held as a loop ("Every one of these animals has the same weakness. Knowing what it is can save your daughter.") → the proof story first → the "why do you think" question that names the weakness ("Because they rely on nobody noticing.") → "Noise is predator repellent" → the pattern line (the girls who made the most noise survived) → his girls → product (Reed reordered my version, 2026-09-29: proof before explanation). The red zone stat goes right after the safety delusion ("It's never the place you're worried about"), before "That alarm is the one thing you can still do before," so the regret beat stays the last line before the share CTA (Reed, 2026-09-29).
- **A real news case (RP41, 2026-09-29):** the detective reacts from outside ("I wasn't on this case, I'm going off what's been reported"); with no charges, every claim is "accused," "allegedly," "according to her lawsuit," and nobody accused is named. When the case facts don't fit the mechanism (she was drugged), say so in his mouth ("If she's already out cold, a keychain can't help her. It's for the minute before.") and let a second, invented girl prove the minute before. The turn from outrage to action: "three chances to protect her after, and all three failed. You only get one chance before."
- **The news peg carried through the whole script (RP50, Reed, 2026-10-07):** three moves. (1) Attention: the hook's "what the news won't say" pays off with one secret, and the secret is the mechanism ("Everybody's asking why seven guys got an essay. Nobody in that house heard a thing, so it was her word against seven."). (2) Relevance: kill "that's not my daughter" with the case's own facts and the narrator's own girl ("Her own sorority is named in the lawsuit too." · "My daughter's not a party girl" · "This isn't a Cornell problem. It's a Friday night problem."). (3) The close brings the case back so the CTA is its ending ("Those seven got an essay. The guy at my daughter's party got arrested. The only difference was a sound." · "That Cornell party was in October." · "…so their girl is the one who gets heard.").
- **The mother version of the news peg (RP50 redo, Reed, 2026-10-09):** her own daughter's story mirrors the case (a party, a cup, upstairs, the door), and the mechanism is ONE told beat from one authority tied to the peg ("The reason the Cornell 7 aren't behind bars yet is because there were no witnesses… That alarm is the only reason we can put these guys behind bars."). No second authority, no "why do you think" stack, no restated payoff block when the scene already showed it; Reed cut all of that as fluff.
- **The two-girl detective shape (Reed, RP40, 2026-09-29):** lead = the case of the girl who lost, told short and stated plainly (white van, the spray taken and used on her, "Those monsters gang raped and killed her," breaking the news to her mother) then the gap ("nobody ever tells her how to actually prevent it") then an open loop naming the payoff ("I'm about to expose the number one predator weakness") then a summarized success story in RP31's form (a girl with an alarm, the pull, heads turn, he's gone, the plate) then the detective's simple breakdown of why it worked, then the mechanism, then the CTA. Strongest when the two girls share one predator ("it was the same white van"), so the rescue closes the first case and "the only difference between the two girls was what was on their keys" is literally true.
- **The alarm is planted neutral: no eye roll, no "ridiculous," no "I made fun of her" (Reed, 2026-09-22, twice: the eye roll cut on RP31, "and I made fun of her for it" cut on RP36, "ridiculous" cut on RP38).** The plant is a fact and nothing else: who put it where and when ("Her bag was in the open locker next to her, and there's a little alarm clipped on the strap. I put it there in August."). A mocking reaction plants "kids won't want it" in the buyer's head, and she acts on that belief at checkout, so the joke costs a sale. The one reaction allowed is the misfire question, and it lives in the CTA, not at the plant: "she was scared it would go off in class, but it's got this feature so it doesn't." Nothing else about how the daughter felt about the alarm goes on the page.
- **Separate the category before you name the brand (Reed, RP33, 2026-09-20).** The junk-alarm beat comes first and it is about alarms in general, not about us: "But be careful buying alarms, the cheap colorful ones from Amazon are garbage. I've had them come to me in evidence bags." Then the personal standard, "I don't cheap out on my daughter's safety." Only then the name, as the exception he trusts: "The only one I actually trust is a small company called Palace Guard." The buyer decides what a good alarm is before she hears who makes one, so the name arrives as the answer to a question she already has.
- **Multiple units for ONE daughter, not one each for five people (Reed, RP33, 2026-09-20, for AOV).** "I put it on my daughter's keys, bags, luggage, and I have a couple spares. You never know when she will need it, so you need to make sure it's always in reach." The old roll-call bundle ("one for her roommate, one for my mother") sells one unit per person and caps the order; the coverage version makes five units the minimum for one girl, and the buyer adds her own people on top.
- **The authority's close always carries the second location (Reed, RP30, RP31, RP32):** "By the time they were at that second location it was too late." It's his line three times running.
- **The mother acts on her anger (Reed, RP32):** she goes to the institution and says it to their face ("I'm not proud of everything I said in that office. I'm not sorry either."), and she's in the courtroom when he's sentenced. The buyer's anger gets somewhere to go.
- **Complete the story (Reed, RP32):** how he got caught, how he got her name, where the daughter is now ("she still gets a little spooked in empty buildings, but she's good"). A skeptical mother asks each of these; answer them in one line apiece.
- **The three standing beats in every story (Reed, 2026-09-18):** Defaults Reed offered, not locks: on RP32 he cut the mother's rules and the spray himself. Write them in; if he cuts them, the cut stands.
  1. **She's the feisty one.** Not shy, loud, could defend herself, shown with one thing she did (broke her brother's nose, the loudest one at dinner). The freeze lands against it, which proves it can happen to any girl, including the buyer's. **The trait can't contradict the trap (Reed, RP58, 2026-10-18).** When politeness is the lure, a mouthy trait ("she'll argue with anybody") contradicts "she didn't want to be rude," so the trait has to be physical, a fighter: "She's done jiu jitsu since she was twelve, and she's put grown men twice her size on the mat." The fighter freezing then shows the training didn't matter.
  2. **The mother is cautious and not dumb, never naive and never controlling (Reed, 2026-10-18, RP57, two passes).** First pass: "a lot of this makes the mother seem naive… we need her to be overly cautious." Second pass, the correction: "I don't mean overprotective and bad. I just mean she's not dumb… she doesn't have to check her phone every night… she's not super controlling, because a lot of the moms aren't like that either." So: she notices the warning signs and acts like a normal careful parent (asks twice, sends the email, has the rule: "you are never alone behind a closed door with a grown man, I don't care who he is"), and she does not surveil (no nightly phone checks, no tracking, no sitting outside). The predator beats her by teaching the daughter to go around her: the daughter knows exactly what Mom would say, so she doesn't ask, and covers it ("I'm studying at the library"); at 17 she drives herself. The authority closes it: "Every one of those girls had a careful mom. He teaches them to go around you… You did everything right, and you still couldn't be in that room." Never "I thought that was so thoughtful," "I didn't think twice," "I loved him too": the buyer is careful, and a naive mother lets her think it couldn't happen to hers. Never the surveillance mom either: most buyers aren't, and she reads as the problem. On RP57 Reed cut the rule line by hand; asking twice and the email were enough. The rule is one option, not a required beat. **Shown by her actions, never said (Reed, RP57, third pass: "a mom wouldn't really say that, they just imply it by their actions").** No "I'm not a helicopter mom," "I'm not dumb," "I'm not a naive mom," and no recap of what she did ("I had the rule, I asked the questions, I sent the email"); the rule, the two questions and the email on the page are the proof, and only the authority may name it ("you did everything right").
  3. **In college stories, the pepper spray was taken,** so she had nothing on her at the time. Believable ways: the dorm's no-weapons rule, an RA room check, TSA on the flight to school, the campus handbook.
- **The selling engines to layer in, without overdoing it (2026-09-18, first used all together on RP32):** take away every other safeguard on screen until the alarm is the only thing left · "nobody told us," with a picture (the alert that went to her spam folder) · the mother's own excuses said as what she used to believe (Reed cut the four-excuse version on RP32; if used, one excuse, one line) · the daughter's life stage in the first ten seconds · how fast it goes, one number · she almost didn't buy it ("it sat in my cart from July") · the girl who didn't have one, named by the same setup and the other ending · the date on the calendar (move-in to Thanksgiving; dark at five after November 2nd) · no friction: she clips it on herself, the daughter asks for more for her friends · the price against what it bought, once. Proven so far: only "nobody told us" and the life stage (RP19). One line each; a story that tries to hit every one in full reads like an ad.
- **When the alarm ends it, a witness is planted before the pull (Reed and me, RP36, 2026-09-22).** Attention needs someone to attend. An alarm in an empty lot with nobody in earshot summons no one, and claiming otherwise is a capability the product can't back. So the story names who is within earshot before the pin comes out (a cleaning van behind the building, a dorm with twenty people, two servers in a doorway), and the pull is what brings them out. If the place is empty on purpose, plant the witness. **And if the car could have made the noise, say why it couldn't** (RP36: the battery cable pulled off, so no horn, no car alarm, no fob).
- **The last beat before the regret line is the safety delusion, in her own voice (Reed, RP31, RP35, RP36; a rule now):** "Keep in mind all of this went down in a safe neighborhood, in the nice part of town. I always worry about things, but I wouldn't in a million years think anything would actually happen." Not a season, not a demographic, not a clock; her own denial, admitted, so the viewer hears her own. Reed on my calendar version: not relatable, doesn't shift the belief as much.
- **Kill the car-noise objection in the scene, then explain it (Reed, RP36):** "I slammed on my car horn but it wouldn't go" at the peak, the cable pulled off in the reveal. The viewer's objection dies before she forms it.
- **The cart beat is not a default any more (Reed, RP36).** He cut "the cheap colorful ones in my cart and my mother says don't" and let the detective carry the junk-ones line, with the name read off the object she already had. Offer the cart beat only on a script with no authority in the back half.
- **The safety delusion can sit before the product as the bridge into "I almost didn't get it" (Reed, RP39, 2026-09-23).** "Remember, our neighborhood was safe and she was with a strong boy, the fact that any of this still happened, I, I just can't believe it. But that's the thing, the detective even said it himself, it always happens when you least expect it, when your daughter's guard is down. That alarm is the only reason Kenzie's sleeping tonight, and I almost didn't get it." The failed safeguard (the boyfriend) is killed inside that line in one clause, not in its own beat; the ending then runs not affiliated → regret → "Don't wait." The mother's rule can be delivered at the moment instead of planted early, if it comes with where she got it (the podcasts).
- **A scream is only invisible where screaming is expected (Reed, RP39, 2026-09-23).** "A kid yelling help at an empty park at night, I promise someone is looking. An alarm isn't exclusive noise, only in RP37 scenarios." The pool line works because two hundred kids are already screaming. Anywhere else, the predator has to take the voice away himself before the alarm is the only noise left: the hand over the mouth (RP33, RP34, RP36), the cable pulled so the horn is dead (RP36), or "if either of you screams, I cut his throat" and the duct tape on both mouths (RP39). Never write "she screamed and nobody came" in a quiet place.
- **Duct tape, not zip ties (Reed, RP39).** Zip ties can be broken and the viewer knows it; a roll of tape does wrists and mouths with one object, and the tape she puts on her own mouth is the picture.
- **The bluff-call mechanism, the third one after the freeze and helplessness (Reed, RP39, 2026-09-23).** He would have killed the boy, but a trafficker doesn't kill the girl, he rapes her and sells her, so the knife in her back is a bluff and she can read it: he's the paranoid one, looking over his shoulder, hurrying, more scared of being seen than she is of him. That read plus the mother's planted rule ("if a man ever tries to put you in a car, you do not get in the car; whatever happens right there is better than what happens wherever he's taking you") is why she's willing to take the risk. She pulls it where she can see the van but he can't yet throw her in it (forty feet), not at the door. Brave, not frozen; said by the detective after ("she called his bluff") and confirmed by the object (the mattress, the chain).
- **The helplessness mechanism, the alternative to the freeze (Reed, RP36).** For a mother with small children the question isn't whether she'd scream, it's that nothing she could do was safe: a knife she didn't know about, kids in car seats, pepper spray that makes him angry, and a man who doesn't leave when he's angry, he gets in the back seat with her kids and she drives him wherever he says. The alarm is the only move that doesn't escalate. Said by the detective, after.
- **Map the script before writing a word (Reed, 2026-09-21).** One row per section: the single belief it kills, the engine that kills it, the proof it uses, the line it exits on, and a word budget. A section that doesn't kill a belief is cut before it's written, which is what keeps a script from running long for no reason. The mechanism rows are fixed (the RP33 block above) and only the story rows change, so the map is mostly a budget exercise: RP33 shipped at 855 words and about four and a half minutes, and that is the benchmark for a detective script. Budgets that worked on RP33: hook 25 · the case 90 · the kit and the blessing 90 · the fight fails 70 · thesis and witnesses 45 · attention as arrest 80 · the throat grab and the slogan 45 · the alarm and the guardrail 90 · pattern and counterfactual 50 · junk alarms into the name 110 · specs, not a weapon, anti-misfire 90 · bundle, company, guarantee, not affiliated 100 · the arrest 45 · clock, new place, identity 60 · regret and CTA 40. The story only has three jobs in this vehicle: make the danger real, show her whole kit failing, and put witnesses a named distance away. Anything else the story wants to do is a different ad.
- **Premortem the big three before the draft goes on the page (Reed, 2026-09-22: "so I don't have to do them myself"; again 2026-10-16 on RP51: "premortem the script every time and automatically apply the big 3 before I need to ask"). Second miss, so it's a gate, not a habit: every draft and every revision gets the pass before it touches Notion, and the chat reply says in one line what it cut. Cut boring and restated lines, never for runtime.** After the map and before Notion, I read my own draft as the audit would and fix it, line by line: Simpler (the same idea twice, narrator overhead, a setup that deletes with nothing lost), Visual (an abstraction where an object could be, a simile where the thing itself would do, jargon a mother doesn't own), Mannerism (my fragment stacks, tidy triads, a "not X, Y" past budget, product-page words, anything she wouldn't say to one listener). Reed's RP38 cuts were all big-three misses I could have caught: the simile "like a piece of the dark was moving," the restated bridge "she wasn't sure, and she pulled it anyway," "doing nights" next to "walking out at eleven." What I changed in that pass is said in chat in one line, not on the page.
- **Story-vehicle pre-flight (every mistake from RP28–RP31, run before the draft goes to Reed):**
  1. Belief ledger first. List what the scene already shows; nothing after the turn restates it (above).
  2. Set up the trait the freeze breaks (broke her brother's nose, the loudest one at dinner, not a shy person), once, early.
  3. No backstory at the peak, and no plant before it (Reed, RP51, 2026-10-16: "delay the intro as long as possible"). The alarm's first mention in the whole script is at the pull. The alarm exists in one line ("there's a little alarm on them"), then the pull. Where it came from goes after the reveal (Reed moved it on RP30 and asked for it on RP31: twice, so it's the rule).
  4. The authority never arrives. A husband who grew up with a deputy (RP30) or a detective on the phone two days later (RP31); borrowed authority, one hop.
  5. The mechanism lives at the close, not mid-story (Reed, RP31 and RP32): after the story, the detective's one thing "every parent needs to hear": she froze, that happens, she didn't need to stop him, the only thing that stops them is their own decision and she makes it for them by making a scene. Mid-story, at most a single picture of why he left, and Reed has cut even that twice.
  6. Product facts happen to her (the cart, the order, the class she was scared of), never a list.
  7. No guardrail line unless Reed writes it (write.md inventory).
  8. The hook's threat is "predator," and the hook keeps the method for the body (hooks.md).
  9. No opener twice ("I'll be honest," "Keep in mind," "Now,") and no line that reads written ("rode around on my purse strap for a month of errands").
  10. Named real companies are fine as places; an accusation against one (a manager who knew) is flagged once, then Reed's call (RP31: kept).
- **Body and CTA:** the structure repeats. Turn → attention → freeze → "so she needs something that makes the noise for her" → the alarm → not a weapon → garbage ones → why Palace Guard → proof → guarantee and not affiliated → bundle → clock → **the parent-regret beat** → close. Reorder only when Reed does.
- **The parent-regret beat ends every script (Reed, 2026-09-14): "the nail in the coffin."** The authority has sat with the parents who didn't buy: "I've sat with those parents, mothers and fathers who would've given anything to have put one of these on their daughter's keys" (RP18) · "The detective said he sat with some who never got theirs back" (RP21) · "I'm, I'm one of them now" (RP19). It comes after the clock and before "Don't wait until it's too late." Never skip it; a script without it isn't finished.
- **The dream-outcome story gets full context (Reed, 2026-09-14):** who she is, when, where he got her, the hand over the mouth, the freeze, the keys in her hand, the windows one by one, the patrol, the call, her first words.
