# RUNBOOK — Audit
> Trigger: "audit" · "audit the full script" · "check" · "review" · "what would you change." Output: typos fixed on the page silently, then the teardown in chat in the fixed order below. The script is Reed's; I tear it apart and hand back options. I never rewrite his lines uninvited.

> 🚨 Before any write: re-read the script file in `brands/<brand>/scripts/`. Reed's manual edits override my plan. If a line I meant to change was edited by Reed, ask first.

## Before reading a line
1. **Re-read the script file** (or the draft Reed pasted). Never audit a prior turn's copy. `git diff brands/` shows what Reed changed since; log those first (CLAUDE.md principle 9).
2. **Load:** the persona register (the brand's `brand.md`), `write.md` for the buyer, the offer and the beat bank, the newest product block for this persona (the brand's `brand.md` names it; the brief itself is in `briefs.md`), the swipe if this is a transposition.
3. **Name the goal in one sentence:** what must the BUYER (the mother, not the daughter) believe and feel to buy this?

## Layer 1 — Auto-fix on the page, then report (no permission needed)
Apostrophes in contractions (`dont`→`don't`, `im`→`I'm`, `Whats`→`What's`) · homophones · missing articles · mid-sentence capitals (`Bout Eighteen`→`bout eighteen`) · terminal punctuation · obvious typos (`image`→`imagine`) · `$`, `%`, `/` in the Script → spelled out (digits themselves are fine, Reed 2026-09-21) · ALL-CAPS emphasis → lowercase · any `[tag]` → removed · name consistency (`Abby`/`Emma`) · two characters whose names sound alike in a spoken script (Katie/Ellie, JC34) → flag, Reed picks the new one.
**The word rule is a fix, not a flag (Reed, 2026-09-29):** "rape" in any form and "sexually assault" become "assault"/"assaulted" (or "abducted" where the line is about taking her) on the page and get reported; a line that says "killed," "murder" or "killers" is flagged at the top of the fix list with implied options ("we never found her," "she never came home," "the men who took her"), because rewording it changes the line.
**Never auto-fix:** rewording, restructuring, word choice, anything that changes meaning. Fragments that land an emotion stay. Spoken grammar beats textbook grammar.

## Layer 2 — The teardown, in this order
**1. What's working.** Name the lines and the moves. Three to five bullets. Be specific so Reed knows what not to touch.

**2. Belief-shift chain.** *In a story vehicle, add a column: shown in the scene, or told after it. Any belief marked both is a restatement and the told line gets cut (JC28 detective scene, JC30 back half).*  Table: belief walking in → the beat that kills it → status (done / weak / missing). Include the exits the viewer wants: "not my daughter's school," "mine would fight back," "she has pepper spray," "someone would hear," "she'd tell me," "I'll do it eventually." Mark where the product appears; it must come after the last belief falls. Flag any belief that's argued twice or explained after the story already proved it.

**3. Gaps, contradictions, fallacies.** In a story vehicle, run write.md §5's story-vehicle pre-flight first; each of its ten items is a finding if it fails.  Sequence errors (froze, then reached for the spray). Facts that changed mid-script (name, date, who said the line). Claims the BODY makes and doesn't pay off (a hook may overpromise; that is never a finding, Reed JC30). A callback pointing at a line that was cut (the brochure on the fridge). Whose mouth a line is in when the speaker shifts. Meta leaks ("the swipe," "the original version").

**4. Beats from the winners.** Check against the live mechanism (write.md §3, JC33: the kit is for a fight he never gives her · she fought and it didn't matter · she didn't need to win it · the witnesses and the distance · attention as arrest · the throat grab as proof · noise is predator repellent · the instinct respected then replaced · the one-sentence counterfactual · proof by pattern) and the CTA bank (junk alarms before the brand name, a true hundred and thirty, not a weapon, anti-misfire, the coverage bundle, the detective gal's small batches, the guarantee, not affiliated, the clock, the regret beat). Name what's missing and where it would sit. The parent-regret beat is mandatory at the end of every script (Reed, 2026-09-14); its absence is always the top fix. The dream-outcome story must carry full context (who, when, where, the freeze, the pin, the windows, the patrol, her first words); a short one is a finding. Name what's present but not load-bearing here and could go. The share CTA ("Share this with every mother you know so their daughter doesn't get abducted and raped.") sits between the regret beat and the link on every script; its absence is a fix, same as the regret beat (Reed, 2026-09-29).

**5. The three first principles, as concrete rewrites, never notes.**
   **Delivery shape (Reed, 2026-09-23):** under each heading (Simpler / Visual / Mannerism), a numbered list where every item is three lines: `❌ old line` · `✅ new line` · one line of reasoning. No prose between items. Then the outside-the-box ideas as their own list.
- **Simpler.** Simpler never means more fragments (Reed, JC39, 2026-09-23: "when you simplify I don't want you to fragment too much"). ❌ "Good kid. Seventeen, six foot, plays football." ✅ "He's a good kid. Seventeen, six foot, and he plays football." A rewrite that turns a sentence into a fragment pair is a fail even if it's shorter; keep the subject and the "and." Same idea said twice → once. Setup sentences that delete with nothing lost. Point arrives late in the sentence → first. Five-sentence fact cards → two sentences a person says. Give the line.
- **Visual.** Abstraction → an object or an action. The object has to be one the buyer already owns the picture of: a ref, a bus stop, a juice box, a mop bucket. A hobby's or a job's jargon ("anchors the relay," JC37; "clinicals," JC38) is specific and invisible at the same time. Sixth-grade language unless we know for a fact most mothers understand the word (Reed, 2026-09-22). Run every noun in the setup through it. "Loud" → "an ambulance siren in her pocket." "Volume" → the nurse not looking up from her clipboard. Test each claim: can I picture it?
- **Mannerism.** Would THIS persona say it out loud? Product-page words in a mom's mouth ("non-violent," "deliberate"). Formal constructions ("cannot"). Too-perfect triads. Then what speech has that writing doesn't: one filler, a restart, an aside, an admission. Persona sheet governs: the male detective says "look," "here's the thing," "god"; the female detective says "I'll be honest with you," "my girls," and since JC20 also "here's the thing," "look," "god"; the mom and the female detective are allowed to be unpolished.

**6. Speech pass.** Fragment runs of three or more → joined; a pair is reported, never fixed (the scanner notes a pair and hard-fails only at three, changed 2026-09-15 after it blocked Reed's intentional "He can't work around that. He leaves."). Same sentence shape three times → varied. Marketing vocabulary → cut. Humanity budget: fillers 4–8 spread through the script (um, like, and; Reed 2026-10-16), never in the hook or the product facts; stumbles ≤1 per script unless the persona is deliberately unpolished (JC19 mom; JC20 female detective, Reed's call); over budget → say so once, then respect Reed's call. Hook ends on a sentence boundary; first body line works after every hook. Word count ÷ 190, stated as information only. Length is never a finding: a video isn't too long, it's too boring (Reed, 2026-09-14). If a stretch drags, name the stretch and make it atomic; never propose cuts for runtime.

**7. Premortem.** Assume it flopped. Rank the kill reasons (never "length"; a boring stretch, named, instead): hook didn't stop the right person · weakest link in the chain · awareness mismatch in the opening · mechanism disbelief · Meta rejection (image is what gets rejected, not script; absolutes are allowed and "rape" cleared spoken and on the plate on JC39, 2026-09-23; the only copy rule is no taboo word in the Primary Text) · persona saturation. Cheapest hedge for each.

**8. Fix list, ranked, lettered.** Highest leverage first. Each problem gets its own a/b/c options. Reed picks letters. "Apply all" means everything in this list; "apply 1, 4" means those numbers.

**9. Outside the box.** One or two only: a beat from a sibling brief, a visual demo, a contrarian framing. Skip if nothing real.

## Rules that override everything above
- **Net-positive only.** If a suggestion doesn't make the line sound more like the person and land harder on the buyer, don't make it.
- **Two-pass gate on every line I propose:** simpler? more visual, spoken-not-read, right for a mother in her fifties or sixties? If Reed has to say "make this more natural," the gate failed.
- **Don't flag these:** a mother-centered story with no bridge to the daughter (Reed, JC30: "it's going to attract mothers still") · a hook that overpromises or doesn't tie perfectly to the body (Reed, JC30: the hook's job is to get them watching, nobody remembers it by the end) · a run of three fragments that sounds like a person talking ("First of all. Hell no. Red flag." — "my mom would say that"; the scanner notes it, four is the fail) · a natural repeated word ("So that's what I bought. I bought three") · a common idiom that still puts a picture in her head ("my blood ran cold" is visual and good) · three hooks stacked at the top (pre-distribution staging) · a detail reused from another brief (a road, a store, a first name) unless it is the load-bearing element of a live ad (Reed, 2026-09-20: "it doesn't matter that I-70 is JC28's road") · a script with no scene of the product working (Reed, 2026-09-20: JC19 carries the account and never shows it working; the mechanism argument and a pattern line are enough) · "second location" as jargon (Reed, 2026-09-20: it makes sense to a parent) · a line reused from another brief, however often (Reed, JC36, 2026-09-22: "it doesn't matter that it was said in other videos, that's not how Meta works"); each ad is seen alone, and no beat is gated to a persona ("it's not earned in a detective's mouth, she can say it too") · a fabricated-but-harmless stat ("number one place predators find women") · absolutes · the "not affiliated" line · filler repeated across ads · the same product block reused · small human contradictions in the persona's voice ("every girl in this family" after "roommates") · deliberate vagueness that is the persona's age talking ("a younger girl," JC21) · a deliberate fragment pair that lands an emotion ("He can't work around that. He leaves." JC22): the scanner flags every run, I report it once and never fix it; the roller coaster wants them · an open loop answered on the next line (Reed, JC22) · a clock or beat that doesn't match the hook's cohort is a finding against the beat, never a reason to add a college clock under a younger-girl hook · dramatized details on a real case, as long as the body keeps the accusation as charged and nothing invented is credited to the report or the warrants; a hook may state it plainly (Reed, JC20). Flag ONLY dangerous advice, a product-capability claim beyond spec, a 100% guarantee, or a checkable false claim about a real person or institution. Flag once, then respect the call.
- **Placeholders in parentheses are decision points, not gaps.** Offer options; don't fill them unless the answer is obvious from a winner.
- **Log Reed's manual edits as patterns.** When he changed something I wrote, say what the change teaches and add it to `edits.md` as a ❌/✅ pair.
- **The big three run on my own draft before it goes on the page, not after Reed edits it** (Reed, 2026-09-22; the pass is written out in write.md §5). If he has to cut a simile, a restated bridge or a doubled fact, the premortem was skipped.
- **A mocked plant is a fix, not a flag.** "She rolled her eyes," "she thought it was ridiculous," "I made fun of her for it" at the moment the alarm is planted → the reaction comes off and the plant is left as a fact (who, where, when). It tells the buyer her kid won't want it. The misfire question in the CTA is the only reaction allowed (Reed, 2026-09-22, cut on JC31, JC36 and JC38; write.md §5).
- **An option on the page must be a different line, not the same line minus three words.** Small rewrites get quoted in chat; the page only gets new beats or real alternatives (Reed, JC33, 2026-09-20).
- **Report every change I made on the page**, and what I considered and left alone.
