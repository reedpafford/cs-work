# RP11 — The rose on her windshield (the movie)
Brand: palace-guard · Persona: daughter (lead and close) · Awareness: UA · Format: Movie
Status: drafting
Parent: RP6 · Lever: L6 Format (Movie)
Cast: Madison (19, a sophomore in Miami) · Brianna (22, Atlanta) · Tyler (Brianna's boyfriend, on FaceTime) · the woman in Phoenix (seen only through her empty car) · Kayla (24, closes at a grocery store, the lead) · Dee (Kayla's coworker) · Kayla's mom (50s) · Walt (70s, the regular who flirts with Kayla at the register) · the man (black Suburban, never says a word) · the cart kid · two women leaving the store · the EMT · two officers · Detective Shaw (50s, flat and quiet) · the news anchor

## Memo
Why: RP6 tells the rose story as a narrated news report; the movie format (RP5, RP9, RP10) lets the viewer watch it happen, girl by girl, and the sedative on the petals is a trap nobody's seen a movie about. · What: we follow each girl as she finds a rose on her windshield and smells it; the fifth, Kayla, sees the others on the news on her break, laughs it off, finds a rose that night, thinks it's from the old man who flirts with her, and the alarm on her keys keeps screaming after she can't. · How: RP6's story, beat for beat, as an anthology in RP10's movie shape (the hooks first, then the body, the detective at the bed, the daughter's V.O. close to the mother).
Avatar: mothers and grandmothers 45 to 70 of a daughter 16 to 25 · Sub-avatar: the mother of a daughter who works late and walks out to her car alone.
Desire: her daughter coming home from a closing shift every night.
Angle: these guys get creative; the rose is just this month's trick, and next month it'll be something nobody's seen yet, so you can't warn her about every trick, you can only make sure she's prepared (Reed: the story isn't the rose, it's the crooks getting creative).
Mechanism: when she's going under, nothing that needs her works (the pepper spray needs a hand, the scream needs a voice); one pull and the alarm keeps going after she can't.
Authority: Detective Shaw, with the other four cars (the door open, the rose on the seat, the pepper spray still in the purse) and the parking lot footage.
Route: Movie (craft/formats/movie.md); hooks open in drama or curiosity, never in the action.

## Body
### Script
[SCENE 1 — a parking garage on a college campus in Miami, a Wednesday night]
(Madison, nineteen, walks to her car with her backpack on one shoulder and her phone to her ear)
Madison: No, I'm done, I'm literally walking to my car. I'll pick up tacos.
(she drops her phone in her purse)
(a red rose is tucked under her windshield wiper)
(she stops; there's no note, just the rose)
Madison: Okay. Who are you?
(she looks around the garage; there's nobody)
(she slides it out from under the wiper, and like anybody would, she smells it)
(she gets in, lays the rose on the passenger seat, and starts the car, still smiling)
(she puts it in reverse, and stops)
(her hand slides off the gear shift)
(the concrete pillars in the windshield start to bend)
Madison: Whoa. Okay.
(she reaches for her purse on the floor; her fingers won't close on the strap)
(two spots over, the door of a black Suburban opens)
(a man gets out and walks toward her door, not in a hurry)
(her eyes close)
(cut to black)

[SCENE 2 — the same garage, the next morning]
(a silver car with the driver's door hanging open, the dome light still on)
(the red rose lies on the driver's seat)
(an officer crouches by the open door; a second officer walks up behind him)
Officer 2: Whose car?
Officer 1: Madison Reyes. Nineteen. Her roommate called it in at six.
(he lifts her purse off the floor mat and looks inside)
Officer 1: Phone's here. Wallet's here. Her pepper spray's still in the purse. She never even got it out.
(the second officer looks at the rose)
Officer 2: Who leaves a rose?
(cut to black)
(time card: six days later, Atlanta)

[SCENE 3 — a gym parking lot in Atlanta, after dark]
(Brianna, twenty-two, walks out of spin class with a water bottle, still out of breath)
(a red rose is tucked under her wiper)
(she grins and FaceTimes her boyfriend)
Tyler (on FaceTime): Hey, babe.
Brianna: Okay, you are so cute. When did you even come by here?
Tyler (on FaceTime): Come by where?
(she holds the rose up to the camera and smells it)
Brianna: Stop. The rose.
Tyler (on FaceTime): Bri, I've been at work since three. What rose?
(she lowers it and looks around the lot)
(she gets in her car and pulls the door shut)
Brianna: Ty, that's not funny. If it's not you, then who
(the phone slides out of her hand onto the floor mat; on the screen, the ceiling of her car)
Tyler (on FaceTime): Bri? Bri, you there?
(two spots over, a black Suburban)
(cut to black)

[SCENE 4 — a hospital parking lot in Phoenix, sunrise]
(a white car with the driver's door open)
(a hospital badge on a lanyard lies on the seat, and a red rose on top of it)
(a phone on the floor mat lights up: Mom)
(it rings)
(it keeps ringing)
(nobody comes)
(cut to black)

[SCENE 5 — a grocery store in Ohio, the register, a Thursday night, an hour to close]
(Kayla, twenty-four, in a green apron, rings up a banana and a lottery ticket)
(her keys hang off a hook by the register, a pink alarm on the ring)
(Walt, in his seventies, leans on the counter like he does every night)
Walt: Ten bucks says I win tonight. And when I do, I'm taking you to dinner.
Kayla: Walt, you say that every night.
Walt: If I was forty years younger, I'd be bringing you flowers.
Kayla (laughing): Goodnight, Walt.

[SCENE 6 — the break room, her last break]
(the local news plays on a TV up in the corner)
(Kayla eats a yogurt; Dee, her coworker, scrolls on her phone across the table)
News Anchor (on the TV): A fourth woman is missing tonight, this time outside Nashville. And police say in every case, they found the same thing on the driver's seat.
(Kayla looks up)
News Anchor (on the TV): A single red rose.
Dee: That is so creepy.
Kayla: That's Miami, though. It's always Miami.
Dee: Atlanta. Phoenix. Nashville's like four hours from here, Kayla.
Kayla: Okay, well, trust me, nobody's leaving me roses.
(her phone buzzes: Mom)
Kayla: Hi, Mom.
Mom (on the phone): Are you watching the news?
Kayla: Mom, I'm at work.
Mom (on the phone): Just, do you still have that alarm on your keys?
Kayla (rolling her eyes at Dee): Yes, Mom. It's on my keys. It lives on my keys.
Mom (on the phone): Text me when you're in the car. Please.
Kayla: I will. Love you.

[SCENE 7 — the parking lot, a little after ten]
(Kayla walks out in her apron, her keys in her hand; the lot's almost empty)
(a red rose is tucked under her wiper)
(she stops)
(she looks back at the store and shakes her head, smiling)
Kayla: Walt.
(she slides it out, and like anybody would, she smells it)
(she gets in her car and drops the rose and her keys in her lap)
(she types to Mom: in the car)
(the letters on her phone start to swim)
(she blinks hard; the steering wheel tilts)
(her phone slides off her leg)
(a flash: the break room TV, "a single red rose")
(she looks down at the rose in her lap)
(two spots over, the door of a black Suburban opens)
(a man gets out and walks toward her door, not in a hurry)
(she opens her mouth to scream, and nothing comes out)
(her hands feel like somebody else's; she gets two fingers through the pin on her keys)
(she pulls)
(the siren screams across the lot, and the strobe flashes white on the man's face)
(he stops ten feet from her door)
(her eyes close; her head tips against the window)
(the alarm keeps screaming)
(the strobe keeps flashing)
(at the cart corral, the cart kid turns, then runs)
Cart Kid: Hey! Hey!
(two women come out the front doors and stare)
(the man turns around, walks back to the Suburban, and drives off)
(the cart kid reads the plate out loud while he types it into his phone)
Cart Kid: T, R, K, four, four, one. T, R, K, four, four, one.
(the alarm is still screaming in her lap)
(cut to black)

[SCENE 8 — the back of an ambulance]
(red lights; Kayla's eyes open)
EMT: There she is. Kayla, can you hear me?
Kayla (slurring): The rose.
EMT: The police have it, honey. Don't worry about the rose.
(on the cot beside her hand, her keys; the pin's gone)

[SCENE 9 — the hospital room, the next morning]
(Kayla in the bed; her mom in the chair, holding her hand)
(the detective knocks on the open door)
Detective: Kayla? I'm Detective Shaw. You mind if I sit?
(she nods; he pulls a chair up to the bed)
Detective: The petals were soaked in a drug. You smell it, you get in your car, and in about a minute your arms get heavy and your eyes won't stay open.
Detective: He doesn't need to fight you. He just waits two spots over until you can't move.
Kayla: I saw it on the news. On my break. And I still smelled it.
Detective: They all smelled it. It's a rose. That's what you do with a rose.
Mom: She thought it was from Walt.
Kayla (quiet): He's this old man who comes through my line. He always says if he was forty years younger, he'd bring me flowers.
Detective: That's why it works. Every girl's got somebody she thinks it's from.
Mom: The girl in Miami, they said she had pepper spray.
Detective: In her purse. We found it there the next morning. She couldn't even lift her arm.
Detective: Look, when you're going under, nothing you're carrying works. Pepper spray needs a hand that works, a scream needs a voice, and you don't have either one.
Detective: The only thing that still works is something that makes the noise for you.
(he turns his laptop around: the parking lot camera, no sound)
(on the screen, the strobe flashes across the lot; Kayla's head is against the window)
(the cart kid runs in from the corral; two women come out the doors)
(the Suburban pulls away)
Detective: You were out before he got to your door. That alarm kept going after you couldn't.
Detective: That kid got his plate. We picked him up at a motel in Indiana around four this morning.
Mom: Oh, thank god.
Detective: But he's not the only one out there. And next month it won't be roses.
Mom: What do you mean?
Detective: These guys get creative. Last spring it was a note stuck on the back window, so she'd stand there and read it. Before that, a car seat left by the dumpster with a blanket over it.
Detective: As soon as one's on the news, they move on to the next thing.
Detective: You can't warn her about every trick. Nobody can. You can make sure she's ready for the one nobody saw coming.
(he looks at Kayla)
Detective: If that alarm had stopped when you did, you'd have been number five.
(Kayla looks at her keys on the tray)
Detective: What kind is that?
Mom: It's, um, Palace Guard. A detective makes them, a woman. I read about it for like a week before I bought it.
Detective: Good. I've had the cheap ones handed to me in evidence bags. A dead battery, or it cracked the first time it hit the pavement.
Detective: Some of those girls carried one for years, and it didn't work the one night they needed it.
Mom: That thing lit up the whole parking lot on the video.
Detective: The witnesses said it was louder than the ambulance that came for her.
(cut to black)

[SCENE 10 — the register, a week later]
(Kayla back at the register in her green apron)
(Walt in line with his banana and his lottery ticket)
Walt: There she is. I heard, kid. You okay?
Kayla: I'm okay, Walt.
(he sets one sunflower on the belt)
Walt: I brought you a flower. Don't you dare smell it.
(she laughs so hard she has to hold the counter)
Kayla (V.O.): I'm twenty-four, I close at the grocery store four nights a week, and my mom still makes me text her when I'm in the car.
(the parking lot at close; Kayla walks to her car with her keys in her hand, the pink alarm swinging)
Kayla (V.O.): I watched the whole story on my break, and I said, that's Miami.
Kayla (V.O.): Then I found a rose on my windshield, and I thought it was from the sweetest old man I know. So I smelled it, like anybody would.
Kayla (V.O.): And next month it won't be a rose. It'll be a flat tire, or a guy who needs a jump, or something nobody's thought of yet.
Kayla (V.O.): You can't warn your daughter about every trick. I'd seen this one on the news, and I still fell for it.
Kayla (V.O.): My mom couldn't be in that parking lot with me. So she put this on my keys, because she'd rather be safe than sorry, and I rolled my eyes at her.
(she gets in her car and texts Mom: in the car)
Kayla (V.O.): And it kept screaming after I couldn't.
(close: the alarm on a key ring next to a car key; a thumb pulls the pin; the strobe fires)
Kayla (V.O.): It's louder than an ambulance siren, it's got a strobe light, and it weighs less than my car key. It won't go off in her purse by accident, either.
Kayla (V.O.): My mom bought three and got two free, so it was less than twenty dollars each. One for my keys, my bag, my car, and, um, two for my cousins. There's a thirty-day guarantee too.
Kayla (V.O.): A detective makes them in small batches, and since this story got out they keep selling out.
Kayla (V.O.): So whatever they come up with next, please make sure she's got one.
Kayla (V.O.): Share this with every mother you know.
Kayla (V.O.): The link's right there, before they sell out again. Don't wait until it's too late.
