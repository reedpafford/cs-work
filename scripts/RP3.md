**Three predators stalked my daughter through Target and pulled a van up right next to her. I almost lost my baby girl over a carton of eggs.**
**Paige is sixteen.** She's tallest in her class, and when she was fourteen, she broke her brother's nose because he grabbed her arm. She's the feisty one.
**I sent her to Target for milk and eggs, right after the sun went down.**
**She noticed three grown men in matching gray sweatsuits, and none of them had a cart.** Weird, but whatever.
Now her first thought was maybe it's just a construction crew or something.
**But every aisle she turned down, one of them was standing at the end of it.**
**Come on.**
**The manager was standing right there, and she didn't say a word.** Mom, I didn't want to be dramatic.
**And** that's on me, I raised her not to make a fuss.
**She paid and walked out to her car, and** a white van with no windows slowly rolls up next to her car, bout three parking spaces away.
Two of them get out, and walk right at her.
And my daughter, who is the loudest one at dinner, could not move. She opened her mouth to scream and nothing came out.
**Her keys were in her hand, and there's a little alarm on them. She dropped the eggs and she pulled it.**
**I've watched the parking lot footage eleven times.**
Right when she pulls her alarm, you just see every head in that parking lot turn at the same time.
**And those two men turn right around, get back in the van, and it's gone. A kid pushing carts got the plate.**
**And the whole time, her little dot was sitting in Target right where it should be, and I was standing at my oven.**
Then she called me twenty minutes later, and I almost passed out.
**Turns out the manager had, like, two weeks of complaints about those same men.**
**A detective called me two days later. That plate matched two other girls.**
**He asked me what she pulled, so I read it off my order, and he said his own girls carry the same one.**
**He told me most of the cheap colorful ones don't make it that far.** I guess he's had different colorful alarms handed to him in evidence bags, the battery was dead, or it cracked the first time it hit the ground.
**I put it on her keys in August when she got her license, and I** didn't think much of it, just a nice to have. Turns out it just saved her life.
She was scared it would go off in class, but that one has this feature so it doesn't misfire, probably the reason she actually carried it.
That night I bought, um, five more and told her to give them to all her friends.
**It's a tiny company called Palace Guard, a detective gal makes them in small batches, I think, and I waited about two weeks because they were sold out.**
**I bought three and got two free, so it was less than twenty dollars each, and there's a thirty-day guarantee, so if she won't carry it, you send it back.**
**And I'm not affiliated with them.** I'm just a mom whose daughter made it home.
If your daughter's got a license now, or she's going off to college, you're not always gonna be there.
And let me be clear, we live in a safe neighborhood, I never thought stuff like this actually happens, let alone happen to my own girl.
**The detective told me about those other two girls.** One of them was safe, he wouldn't talk about the other one.
**And he told me one thing I can't forget.** The only thing that really stops these animals is their own decision, and she can make it for them if she makes herself too risky to grab.
An alarm isn't a force field, but drawing attention is the most powerful defense she has.
