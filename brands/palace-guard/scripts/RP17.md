## Memo
Why: the buyer who already has this fear has been told she's overreacting, by her husband and by her daughter; no movie of ours has put that on screen, and RP13 named the beat we haven't dramatized ("walked home by somebody she met an hour ago"). · What: on the night of a party, intercut, the dad tells the mom she worries too much and the daughter tells her to stop texting, while a nice boy from her class walks her home and won't take no at her dorm door, and the alarm the dad called twenty bucks of paranoia is what stops it. · How: RP10's movie shape (the hooks first, then the body, the expert after), the dramatic-irony intercut from movie.md (the dot, the oven), RP13's walked-home beat.
Avatar: mothers 45 to 65 of a daughter in college · Sub-avatar: the mother who already lies awake over it, whose husband says she watches too much news and whose daughter says "please stop."
Desire: her daughter coming home the same girl she left, and being able to sleep.
Angle: she's watching her own fear play out; the danger isn't a stranger, it's the guy from class who offers to walk her home, and the mom who worries isn't crazy, she's the only one paying attention (Reed: a grounded assault, not an abduction).
Mechanism: unwanted attention; he's counting on just the two of them behind a door, her word against his, and one pull opens every door on the hall.
Authority: the campus police officer in the dorm lounge that night.
Route: Movie (craft/formats/movie.md); the body starts the night of it, no flashbacks, the parents and the daughter intercut, Lisa's V.O. close.

## Body
### Script
[SCENE 1 — Lisa and Mark's kitchen, a Friday night, a little before midnight]
(the local news on the little TV by the microwave)
(Lisa sends Emily a text: Are you back at the dorm yet?)
Mark: How many is that tonight?
Lisa: Three.
Mark: She's nineteen. She's at college. Let her be nineteen.
News Anchor (on the TV): A sophomore at a state university says she was assaulted walking home from an off-campus party. Police say the man she named is a student she knew from class.
(Lisa looks up at the TV)
Lisa: Mark. Somebody she knew from class.
Mark: Lees, I heard it.

[SCENE 2 — a house party off campus, the same time]
(loud music, red cups, a kitchen packed shoulder to shoulder)
(Emily, nineteen, a cup in her hand, looks over the crowd for her roommate)
(her keys hang off one finger, a pink palace guard on the ring)
(her phone buzzes: Mom: Are you back at the dorm yet?)
(she rolls her eyes and types: Mom. I'm fine. Please stop.)
Connor: Em! You came.
(Connor, a junior from her stats class, the guy everybody likes, squeezes through the crowd)
Emily: Hey. Have you seen Jess?
Connor: She left like twenty minutes ago with that soccer guy.
Emily: Are you serious? She was supposed to walk back with me.
Connor: I'll walk you. It's on my way.
Emily: You don't have to do that.
Connor: Em, it's midnight. I'm not letting you walk back by yourself.
(she smiles)

[SCENE 3 — the kitchen]
(Lisa's phone buzzes on the counter: Mom. I'm fine. Please stop.)
(she turns it around so Mark can see)
Lisa: She told me to stop.
Mark: Because you text her four times a night. Keep it up and she's gonna stop answering.
Lisa: Did she even put that alarm on her keys?
Mark: The twenty dollar keychain.
Lisa: Mark.
Mark: Lees, you mailed a nineteen year old a panic button. It's in a drawer somewhere. Twenty bucks of paranoia.

[SCENE 4 — the street outside the party, then across campus]
(Emily and Connor walk out the front door; the music fades behind them)
(she's a little tipsy, her shoes in one hand, her keys hanging off a finger)
Connor: You good?
Emily (laughing): I'm great. I'm so great.
(she steps off the curb wrong; he catches her arm and they both laugh)
Connor: Okay, how many of those did you have?
Emily: Like two. Maybe three. Jess kept handing them to me.
Connor: Jess just ditched you, by the way.
Emily: Jess always ditches me.
(they cut across the dark quad, past the library, the lights off)
Connor: Is she coming back tonight, or is she staying at the soccer guy's?
Emily: Oh, she's gone all weekend. She basically lives there.
Connor: So you've got the room to yourself.
(she laughs, and doesn't answer)
(his arm slides around her waist)
Connor: You know you're really cute when you're drunk.
(she laughs again, smaller this time, and steps out from under his arm)
Emily: Okay. It's right up here.

[SCENE 5 — the kitchen]
(Mark rinses his cup in the sink)
Mark: You know what your problem is? You watch too much of that stuff.
Lisa: It's the news, Mark.
Mark: It's the worst thing that happened in the whole state today. That's not Emily. That's not our kid.
(he kisses the top of her head)
Mark: If something was wrong, we'd know. Come to bed.
(Lisa looks down at her phone: Please stop.)
(she turns off the TV)

[SCENE 6 — the side door of Emily's dorm, after midnight]
(Emily swipes her card; the lock clicks green)
Emily: Okay, this is me. Thank you, seriously.
Connor: Let me come up. Just for a little.
Emily: I'm really tired, Connor.
Connor: Come on. Your roommate's gone all weekend.
Emily: Connor, no. I'm going up by myself.
(she pulls the door open; he puts his hand flat on it, above her head)
Emily: Connor, I'm going to bed.
Connor: You've been flirting with me all night.
Emily: I wasn't. I was being nice.
(he steps into the doorway with her; the stairwell behind her is empty)
(his hand closes around her wrist)
Connor: Don't make this weird, Em. Just be quiet.
(she opens her mouth to scream, and nothing comes out)
(the keys are still on her finger)

[SCENE 7 — Lisa and Mark's bedroom]
(Mark switches off the lamp on his side)
(Lisa on her back in the dark, eyes open)

[SCENE 8 — the stairwell]
(Emily gets two fingers through the pin)
(she pulls)
(the siren fills the stairwell; the strobe flashes white on Connor's face)
(he lets go of her wrist)
(doors bang open on the floor above; somebody yells down the stairs)
RA (O.S.): Hey! What's going on down there?
(an RA in pajama pants comes down the stairs two at a time, three girls behind her)
(Connor backs out the door and runs across the lawn)
(Emily slides down the wall to the floor, the alarm still screaming in her hand)

[SCENE 9 — Lisa and Mark's bedroom]
(Mark asleep; Lisa on her back, eyes open)
(her phone lights up on the nightstand: Emily)
Lisa: Em?
Emily (on the phone, sobbing): Mom. Mom, can you come get me?
(Lisa sits straight up)
Lisa: What happened? Emily, where are you?
Emily (on the phone): He wouldn't let go of me. I pulled the alarm.
(Mark sits up and sees her face)
(he's already pulling on his jeans)
(cut to black)

[SCENE 10 — the dorm lounge, a little after two]
(Emily on a couch in a hoodie, the RA next to her)
(the pink alarm on the coffee table, the pin pushed back in)
(the campus police officer in a chair across from her, a notepad on his knee)
(Lisa and Mark come through the door; Lisa doesn't stop until she's holding her)
(Mark stops at the coffee table and looks at the alarm)
Officer: You're the parents? She's okay. She did everything right.
Emily: He sits two rows behind me in stats. I let him walk me home.
Officer: Look. I've worked this campus fourteen years. It's almost never some stranger in the bushes. It's a guy she knows, from class, from a party, who offers to walk her home. And she says yes, because he's nice, and because every girl on this campus says yes.
Emily: I tried to scream. Nothing came out.
Officer: That happens to almost every girl. The tough ones too. Your body locks up, and it's not weakness, it's just what bodies do.
Mark: What about pepper spray? We can get her pepper spray.
Officer: And where's she keep it? The bottom of her bag. She's had a couple drinks, and he's got her by the wrist. She's not digging for anything.
Officer: Here's the thing. A guy like this is counting on it being just the two of them, behind a door, where it's her word against his.
Officer: That thing on her keys brought him the one thing he didn't want. Attention. She didn't have to say a word, and half that hall had their doors open before he hit the lawn.
Officer: We have his name, the RA knows exactly who he is. But he's not the only one. This is every weekend, on every campus.
Officer: Who got her the alarm?
(Mark looks at Lisa)
Lisa: I did. It's called Palace Guard. A detective gal makes them.
Officer: Good. I've pulled the cheap ones out of evidence bags. Dead battery, or it cracked the first time it hit the sidewalk. This one worked.
Officer (to Lisa): Whatever you did to get her to carry it, keep doing it.
(Mark looks at the floor)

[SCENE 11 — the car, sunrise, on the highway home]
(Emily asleep in the back seat under Mark's jacket)
(Mark drives; Lisa holds Emily's keys in her lap, the pink alarm on the ring)
(a long quiet)
Mark: I called it twenty bucks of paranoia.
(Lisa doesn't say anything)
Mark: I'm sorry, Lees. You were right.
(he keeps his eyes on the road)
Mark: Text her as many times as you want.
(Lisa laughs, and it turns into crying)
Mark: And get one for Katie. And your mom.

[SCENE 12 — the close, a week later]
(Emily at the kitchen table at home in a hoodie, eating cereal; Lisa watches her from the doorway)
Lisa (V.O.): I'm the one in my house who worries. I text four times a night, and I know it drives my daughter crazy.
(Emily's keys on the hook by the door, the pink alarm on the ring)
Lisa (V.O.): My husband told me I watch too much news. My daughter told me to stop.
Lisa (V.O.): And that night she was walking home with a boy from her class. The nice one. The one I never would have worried about.
(Emily backs out of the driveway for campus; Lisa waves from the porch)
Lisa (V.O.): I couldn't be at that door, nobody's mom can. But the thing my husband laughed at was on her keys, and when she couldn't scream, it screamed for her.
Lisa (V.O.): She came home the same girl she left.
Lisa (V.O.): So if you're the one who worries, you're not crazy. You're just the only one paying attention.
(the alarm in a hand: the pin, the strobe)
Lisa (V.O.): It's called Palace Guard. It's louder than an ambulance siren, it's got a strobe light so nobody can pretend they didn't see, and it's lighter than her car key. It's even got an anti-misfire feature, so it won't go off in her bag in the middle of class.
Lisa (V.O.): I bought three and got two free, so it was less than twenty dollars each. One for Emily, one for her sister, one for my mom, and two for her roommates.
Lisa (V.O.): A detective gal makes them in small batches, and they keep selling out.
(Lisa in bed at night, her phone face down on the nightstand, asleep)
Lisa (V.O.): I still text her. But I sleep now.
Lisa (V.O.): Send this to every mom who's been told she worries too much. And send it to her husband, too.
Lisa (V.O.): The link's right there. Please, don't wait until it's too late.
