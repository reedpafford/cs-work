## Body
### Script
I've been doing this for, um, eleven years, and I've got a daughter of my own, so I'm gonna tell you what we don't put in the welcome packet.
On a Friday night, I've got four officers for a campus of twenty thousand kids. Four.
While we're breaking up a party on one side of campus, there's a girl walking back to her dorm alone on the other side, and nobody's watching that walk.
Those blue emergency phones on the poles? Half of them haven't been tested in years, I think, and a girl who's being grabbed isn't running to a pole anyway.
And the stuff we do see, god. Guys sitting in their cars outside the girls' dorms at two in the morning, girls getting walked home by somebody they met an hour ago, a drink left on a table for one minute.
Most of it never gets reported to us. We hear about it weeks later, from a mom on the phone, crying, asking me how this happened on our campus.
Right now we're in what we call the red zone, the weeks between move-in and Thanksgiving, when most assaults on campus happen, and it's, like, mostly freshman girls.
Here's the thing. By the time anybody calls us it's over, because it takes us a few minutes to get there and it takes him about thirty seconds.
Pepper spray helps, but most dorms don't allow it, and a guy twice her size can take it right out of her hand.
What actually stops these guys is attention. He needs her quiet and nobody looking, because if one person sees his face, we've got him.
That's why we tell everyone to carry a Palace Guard alarm.
She pulls one pin and it's a hundred and thirty decibels, like an ambulance going off in her hand, with a strobe light so we can find her, and every window in that dorm lights up.
It's not a weapon, so it's allowed in every dorm, and it clips right on her keys.
I'm not saying it's a force field, but I'd rather get a noise complaint than a call from her mother.
You can get three and get two free, so they come out to less than twenty dollars each.
