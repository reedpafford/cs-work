# RP10 — The cute guy at Target (the movie)
Brand: palace-guard · Persona: daughter (lead and close) · Awareness: UA · Format: Movie
Status: final
Parent: — · Lever: —
Cast: Lily (19, a sophomore at a college three hours from home) · Jen (her mom, late 40s) · the man (26 or 27, a little older than her, very good looking, easy smile; says his name is Jake) · Kayla (Lily's roommate) · Detective Hale (50s, flat and quiet)

## Memo
Why: RP5 proved the movie format on a Target story; this one moves the threat from strangers she clocks to a man she likes, the one no girl would clock. · What: a girl meets a cute guy at Target who was there to pick a girl to abduct that night, and the alarm on her keys is the reason she wakes up in a hospital and not somewhere else. · How: RP5's skeleton (Target, the mom on the phone, the detective with the footage) and the four competitor movies in archive/movie-ads/.
Avatar: mothers and grandmothers 45 to 70 of a daughter 16 to 25 · sub-avatar: the mother of a daughter away at college, who calls to check on her and trusts her judgment about people · Desire: her daughter safe from the danger she won't see coming · Angle: he doesn't look like a predator; he's cute, a little older, and nice to her, and she misjudges him a little, the way any girl would · Mechanism: noise and attention; he needs it quiet and nobody looking, which is why he tells her alarms don't work · Authority: the detective with the parking lot footage
Route: Movie (craft/formats/movie.md); hooks open in drama or curiosity, never in the action (Reed)

## V1 - Moms POV
### Script
[HOOK — Jen's kitchen at home, that night]
(Jen's phone buzzes on the counter: a number she doesn't know)
Jen: Hello?
Nurse (on the phone): Hi, is this Lily's mom? This is Mercy Hospital. Your daughter was brought in about twenty minutes ago.
Jen: What? No, I just talked to her. She was at Target.
Nurse (on the phone): Ma'am, I can't say much over the phone. There's a detective here who needs to talk to you.
(Jen grabs her car keys off the counter and runs)
(cut to black)
(time card: that afternoon)

[SCENE 1 — the Target by campus, the party aisle, late afternoon]
(Lily, in a college sweatshirt, drops a sleeve of red party cups into her basket, on top of chips and paper plates)
(her keys hang off one finger, a pink palace guard on the ring)
(she's up on her tiptoes, reaching for a bag of balloons on the top shelf)
(a hand reaches over her and pulls it down)
Man: Here you go.
(she turns around; he's very good looking, a few years older than her, maybe twenty-six)
Lily (blushing, flustered): Oh. Thanks.
(they look at each other a second too long)
(he smiles and walks off down the aisle)
(she watches him go, then looks down at her basket)

[SCENE 2 — Target, the chip aisle, a few minutes later]
(Lily comes around the end of the aisle and almost runs her basket into his cart)
Man: Oh, hey. We have to stop meeting like this.
Lily: Are you following me?
Man: I was about to ask you the same thing.
(she laughs)
Man: Okay, I'm just gonna say it, you're really cute.
Lily: Oh my gosh.
Man: Sorry, is Target a weird place to
(she cuts him off)
Lily (flustered, stuttering): No, it's, I mean you're cute too, I. Thank you.
(she's playing with her keys while she talks; the little alarm swings off her finger)
Man: Please tell me your boyfriend's not about to come around that corner.
Lily: Boyfriend? Me? No. It's just me and a bunch of solo cups.
Man: Good. I mean, not good. Okay, I'm really bad at this.
(she laughs)
Man: What's the party?
Lily: It's my roommate's birthday tonight. I'm in charge of cups and snacks.
Man: Oh wow, that's a big job.
Lily: Huge. You know what they say. Can’t have a party without cups.
Lily: What are you here for?
Man: Lightbulbs, dish soap, you know. Boring errands. I've been here since noon.
Lily: Since noon? I’m not sure if that’s stupidity, or commitment.
Man: Let’s say commitment. What can I say, I really like Target.
(he holds her eyes; she looks away, smiling)
(he nods at her keys)
Man: Is that one of those alarm things?
Lily: Oh, yeah. My mom got it for me when I left for school. I liked the color and it made her happy to keep it on my keys.
Man: It's a good color, I like it on you. But uh, you know those don't really work, right?
(he says it like a joke)
Lily: Well thankfully I've never had to use it, but thanks for the heads up.
Man: Sorry, that was such a weird thing to say.
Man (stuttering, nervous): Anyway, uh, can I get your Instagram, or maybe a number? Or not, I, you know, up to you.
(Lily laughs and holds her hand out for his phone)
(she types in her name and hands it back)
Man: Lily. Okay. I'm Jake.
Lily: Hi, Jake.
Man: I'll see you around, Lily.
(she walks off and looks back once; he's still smiling)
(she smiles at the floor the whole way to the end of the aisle)

[SCENE 3 — Jen's kitchen at home, three hours away, the same minute]
(Jen unloads the dishwasher with her phone to her ear)
Jen: Hey, baby, I'm just checking on you. How's your week going? Are you eating? Studying hard?
Lily (on the phone): Yes, Mom, I’m fine. I'm at Target getting stuff for Kayla's birthday. I, also, um, met a guy.
Jen: In Target? I met my ex boyfriend at Target. You gotta be careful with those.
Lily (on the phone): Mom, he's so cute. Like, dreamy. We just kept running into each other, and when he asked for my number I
(Jen cuts her off)
Jen: How old is he?
Lily (on the phone): I don't know, like twenty-six? He's a little older. Mom, it's fine.
Jen (playful): Hun, he's still a stranger. Be careful.
Lily (on the phone): Mom, I'm an adult. Besides, he's way too cute to be a creep.
(Jen laughs)
Jen: Right. Text me when you're home. Have fun tonight, I love you.
(she hangs up, still smiling)

[SCENE 4 — Target, the front doors, the sun going down]
(Lily walks out with her bag, smiling at her phone)
(on the screen: a new follower, then a message from him: have fun at the party tonight)
(she bites her lip and types back)

[SCENE 5 — the parking lot, her car in the back row]
(a gray car is parked right next to hers now, with the hood up)
(the man is bent over the engine with his sleeves rolled up)
(he looks up and smiles)
Man: Okay, I swear this isn’t on purpose.
Lily (still playful): Okay, now you’ve got to be following me.
Man: The stupid battery's dead. As if I haven't been here long enough.
(she laughs)
(she looks at the gray car)
(a flash: the same spot, empty, when she pulled in an hour ago)
Lily (V.O.): That spot was empty when I got here.
(a flash: the chip aisle, the man laughing, "I've been here since noon")
Lily (V.O.): He said he's been here since noon.
(she looks back at him; he's still smiling at her)
Man: Hey, can I ask you a huge favor? Any chance you could give me a jump? The cables are in a bag on my passenger seat, if you could grab them. My hands are disgusting.
(he holds up his hands, black with grease, and wipes them on a rag)
(she looks at the passenger door)
(she looks through the passenger window)
(in the back seat, a woman is lying low across the seat, watching her)
(the woman doesn't look away)
(Lily notices, then quickly looks away)
Lily: You know what, I'm so sorry, my roommate's waiting on all this stuff. I should really get going. It was nice to meet you though!
Man: Totally. Yeah, no, go.
(she turns to her car)
Man: Hey, Lily.
(she looks back)
Man: You didn't even give me your number.
Lily: You have my Instagram.
(she smiles, but she doesn't stop walking)

[SCENE 6 — her car]
(she drops into the driver's seat and pulls the door shut)
(it doesn't close)
(his hand is on top of the door)
(he isn't smiling anymore; his face is just flat)
Man: Get out of the car.
Lily: What?
(he opens the door all the way)
(behind him, the back door of the gray car opens, and the woman gets out)
Man: Don't scream. Just get out of the car.
(she begins to panic)
(he grabs her wrist and pulls, and she comes halfway out of the seat)
(her keys are still in her other hand; she gets to the palace guard alarm and pulls the pin)
(the siren screams across the lot, and the strobe flashes white on his face)
(every head in the parking lot turns)
(a buff military dad loading groceries two rows over drops his bags)
(a woman pushing a cart stops dead)
(the man lets go of Lily's wrist, and realizes he needs to go)
(he walks away fast, and the woman scrambles out from behind the gray car and hurries after him toward a silver minivan two rows over)
(the military dad and the woman with the cart jog over to Lily's car)
Military Man: Hey! What's going on over here?
Woman with Cart: Honey, are you okay? Did he hurt you?
(Lily tries to answer; her hand is shaking so hard the alarm rattles against the door)
(the minivan pulls out slow, like nothing happened)
(Lily's eyes go unfocused, and she slumps sideways in the seat)
(the screen gets hazy)
Woman with Cart: Hey, hey, stay with me. Somebody call 911.
(cut to black)

[SCENE 7 — the hospital room, night]
(a heart monitor beeps; Lily's eyes open)
(the Target bag on the chair, the party cups sticking out of it)
Jen (frantic, hugging): Oh, baby, you're okay. I almost lost you. Oh my goodness.
(Jen holds Lily's face in both hands and cries)
Lily: Mom, Kayla's party.
(Jen laughs through it)
Jen: Kayla's out in the hallway. Nobody cares about the party, baby.
(the detective knocks on the open door)
Detective: Lily? I'm Detective Hale. Mind if I sit?
(she nods, looks surprised, he comes in to sit and talk to her)
Detective: The man you met at Target today. He wasn't there to shop. He was there to pick a girl to abduct tonight.
Lily: But he was so nice. And he was hot.
Detective: They're always nice. You'd never expect it.
Lily: He told me his name was Jake.
Detective: His name isn't Jake.
Lily (her memory starts coming back): There was a woman hiding in his back seat.
Detective: She's done this with him before. They had a second car parked two rows over the whole time.
(he turns his laptop around: the parking lot camera, no sound)
(on the screen, the gray car pulls into the empty spot next to Lily's car)
Detective: That gray car was stolen two days ago. He moved it next to yours while you were checking out.
(on the screen, he's at her door; the strobe flashes white across the lot)
(every head in the lot turns at the same time)
(people drop what they're doing and run toward her car)
(he and the woman walk two rows over and get into the minivan)
Detective: The doctor says you passed out from the adrenaline. But you pulled that pin first, and that's why you're in this bed and not in that van.
(Lily nods)
Detective: We’ve been getting more and more of these types of abductions. Most girls either aren’t prepared, or all they’ve got is pepper spray or a weak taser. 
Detective: He's trained for those, he takes them like they’re nothing. He can't train for 40 different people alerted by what he's doing.
Detective: These guys are pros. He spent all day in that store picking. 
Detective: Think about what he asked you. If you had a boyfriend. Where you'd be tonight. If that alarm works. None of that was flirting.
(Lily looks at the ceiling)
Jen: I called her while she was at the store. She told me he was cute, and I made a joke about it. I -
(Jen can't finish)
Jen: It's a nice college town. I didn't think this happened there.
Detective: So did the last two girls.
Jen: Are they okay?
Detective: One of them is.
(he looks at his hands)
Detective: The point is, he doesn't look like what you're picturing. It's not a guy in a van. It's a handsome man, someone who knows you'll trust him. It's all a front.
Detective: These guys know a girl will trust a good-looking guy faster than anybody, so that's who they send in. You can't teach a sweet girl not to be sweet. You can make sure she's prepared.
Detective: Thank god she had that thing on her keys. Most of the girls in my files didn't, and those are the ones we rarely get back.
Detective: Which alarm did you have?
Jen: It's, um, (scrolls on her phone to find it) from a brand called Palace Guard. I got it for peace of mind and because she liked the color.
(he nods)
Detective: Good. You made the right choice. My own girls carry the same one.
Detective: I've had the cheap ones handed to me in evidence bags. Those were the ones he was talking about when he said they don't work. He’s seen it.
Detective: They've all got a dead battery, or they broke the first time they hit the ground.
(watching the footage)
Jen: I didn't even know how strong these were. That thing lit up the parking lot.
Detective: The witnesses said it was louder than an ambulance siren too.
(on the tray by the bed, Lily's keys; pink, the pin gone)
Lily: He told me it was a good color.
(cut to black)

[SCENE 8 — Kayla's birthday party, moved to the next weekend]
(a small apartment full of friends, red cups, music)
(Lily laughs on the couch, a bruise fading on her wrist, her keys clipped to her belt loop)
(her phone buzzes: Mom)
(she steps out into the hallway to answer)
Lily: Hi, Mom. Yes, I'm fine. We’re having a ton of fun.
Lily (V.O.): I'm nineteen, I'm a sophomore, and my mom still calls me every day to ask if I'm eating.
Lily (V.O.): A cute guy tells me I'm cute in the chip aisle, and I blush and give him my Instagram. Of course I did.
Lily (V.O.): My mom told me to be careful, and I told her he was way too cute to be a creep. I would have never thought it would be someone who looked like that.
Lily (V.O.): And when he asked me to grab his cables, I almost did. That's what you do when somebody needs help.
Lily (V.O.): If that sounds like your daughter, it's because he spent all day in that store looking for a girl just like her.
Lily (V.O.): My mom bought this when I left for school. She didn't think anything would ever happen to me. No one thinks anything actually will.
(she laughs at something her mom says and leans her head against the wall)
Lily (V.O.): And it's the only reason I'm still here to pick up when she calls.
(close: the alarm on a key ring next to a car key; a thumb pulls the pin; the strobe fires, flashback fashion)
Lily (V.O.): It clips on her keys, it's as loud as an ambulance, and comes in cute colors. It even has an anti-misfire feature so it won't go off in her purse.
Lily (V.O.): My mom bought three and got two free, so it came out to less than twenty dollars each. There's a thirty-day guarantee too. She checked, because she reads everything now.
Lily (V.O.): A detective makes them in small batches, and they sell out a lot. So if the link still works, they've still got some.
Lily (V.O.): So if your daughter, or your granddaughter, is sweet like me, please get her one. My mom almost didn't.
Lily (V.O.): Share this with every mother you know so their daughter doesn't get abducted or assaulted.
Lily (V.O.): The link's right there. Don't wait until it's too late.
