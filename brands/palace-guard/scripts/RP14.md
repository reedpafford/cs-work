Status: drafting

## Memo
Why: nothing like it on the account or in the category; a recorded call is the closest she can get to hearing it happen to her own daughter, in real time, and true-crime 911 audio is something mothers already watch.
What: a detective plays a 911 call from a girl being abducted, she survives, and he tells us the family said it was a Palace Guard.
How: detective to camera (the setup) · the call as audio over a waveform and captions, nobody on camera · back to the detective: the family, the reveal, the JC33 mechanism and product block.
TAM: parents with girls in college
Avatar: mothers 45 to 65 of a daughter in college · Sub-avatar: the mother whose daughter walks to her car alone after a night class.
Desire: her daughter making it home when somebody grabs her and nobody's there.
Angle: even when she does everything right and calls 911, help is minutes away; the only thing that reaches her in time is a noise.
Setting: a campus parking garage after a night class.
Mechanism (JC33): he needs her quiet and nobody looking; the siren comes through on the call, the dispatcher hears it, and he runs. Noise is predator repellent.
Authority: a detective (the male detective, JC33 register).
Route: a format, new: the detective frame on the Authority runbook, the call on the movie rules (people talk to each other, never to camera).

### Script
**(the detective at his desk, to camera)**
**Detective: This is a 911 call from a nineteen-year-old college girl who was being abducted in her campus parking garage.**
**Detective: Her family let us share it, because they want every parent to hear how fast it happens. Listen to how quiet she is, and listen all the way to the end.**
**(black screen, an audio waveform and word-by-word captions; under it, an empty concrete garage, flickering lights)**
**Dispatcher: 911, what's the address of your emergency?**
**Ashley (whispering): I'm in the parking garage at school, the one behind the science building.**
**Dispatcher: Okay, what's going on there?**
**Ashley: There's a man following me. He's been behind me since I got off the elevator.**
**Dispatcher: Can you tell me what he looks like?**
**Ashley: Gray hoodie. He's big. He's like two cars behind me.**
**Dispatcher: What's your name, hon?**
**Ashley: Ashley.**
**Dispatcher: Okay Ashley, I'm sending officers to you right now. They're about four minutes out. Can you get to your car?**
**Ashley: It's right here, I'm almost there.**
**Dispatcher: Get in, lock the doors, and stay on the phone with me.**
**(keys jingling)**
**Ashley: I can't find the right key.**
**Dispatcher: Is there anybody else around you?**
**Ashley: No. There's nobody on this floor. He's walking faster.**
**Ashley: Oh my god, he's right**
**(a scuffle, and the phone cracks against the concrete)**
**Dispatcher: Ashley? Ashley, can you hear me?**
**The man (muffled, close): Not a sound.**
**Dispatcher: Ashley, officers are on the way. Ashley?**
**(the siren, so loud it clips the recording, bouncing off every concrete wall)**
**Dispatcher (to someone next to her): I've got some kind of alarm going off on the line.**
**Voice (far off, from the level below): Hey! Hey!**
**Second voice: Up here, it's coming from up here!**
**(running footsteps, a van door, tires squealing down the ramp)**
**(the siren keeps going, then stops)**
**Ashley (crying, close to the phone): He's gone. He ran. He had my arm, he was pulling me to his van.**
**Dispatcher: You're okay, Ashley. Officers are pulling in right now. You did so good.**
**Ashley: Can you, can you call my mom?**
**(back to the detective)**
**Detective: Look, abductions like this are a lot more common than most parents realize. I've been doing this twenty-two years, and I've never had as many come across my desk as this fall.**
**Detective: Remember, officers were four minutes out. He needed about thirty seconds.**
**Detective: That's why we've been telling every parent we talk to, get your daughter a personal alarm. Most girls freeze, so she doesn't have to scream. She pulls one pin, and everybody in that garage hears it.**
**Detective: We asked Ashley's family what she had on her keys. It was a Palace Guard, and that alarm saved her life.**
**Detective: Her mom got to pick her up from that garage. A lot of the mothers I've sat across from never got that call.**

**Detective: Tap the link below to get one before they sell out, and share this with every mother you know so their daughter doesn't get abducted or assaulted.**
**Detective: Don't wait until it's too late.**
