# RP10 — The cute guy at Target (the movie)
Brand: palace-guard · Persona: daughter (lead), mother (close) · Awareness: UA · Format: Movie
Status: drafting
Parent: — · Lever: —
Cast: Lily (19, home from college for the weekend) · Jen (her mom, late 40s) · the man (early 30s, very good looking, flannel, easy smile) · the little boy (about six, never smiles) · Detective Hale (50s, flat and quiet)

## Memo
Why: RP5 proved the movie format on a Target story; this one moves the threat from strangers she clocks to a man she likes, the one no girl would clock. · What: a girl meets a cute guy at Target who was there to pick a girl to abduct that night, and the alarm on her keys is the reason she wakes up in a hospital and not somewhere else. · How: RP5's skeleton (Target, the mom on the phone, the detective with the footage) and the four competitor movies in archive/movie-ads/.
Avatar: mothers and grandmothers 45 to 70 of a daughter 16 to 25 · sub-avatar: the mother who trusts her daughter's judgment about people · Desire: her daughter safe from the danger she won't see coming · Angle: he doesn't look like a predator; he's cute, he has a kid, and he's nice to her · Mechanism: noise and attention; he needs it quiet and nobody looking, which is why he tells her alarms don't work · Authority: the detective with the parking lot footage
Route: Movie (craft/formats/movie.md); hooks open in drama or curiosity, never in the action (Reed)

## V1 - Drama start
### Script
[HOOK — a hospital room, night]
(a heart monitor beeps; Lily's eyes open)
(a Target bag sits on the chair by the bed, a box of birthday candles sticking out of it)
Jen: Oh, baby. Hi. Hi. You're okay.
(a detective stands in the doorway with a laptop under his arm)
Detective: Lily, I need to ask you about the man you met today. The one with the little boy.
Lily: The cute one? Mom, I told you about him.
(Jen looks at the detective, then at the floor)
Detective: That little boy isn't his.
(cut to black)

[SCENE 1 — Target, the party aisle, late afternoon]
(Lily, in a college sweatshirt, drops a box of birthday candles into her basket)
(her keys hang off one finger, a little mint-green alarm on the ring)
(she looks up)
(across the aisle, a man is reading the back of a pack of paper plates, and he's very good looking)
(he looks over and smiles at her, like he didn't mean to)
(Lily smiles and looks down at her basket; her cheeks go pink)
(a little boy stands next to his cart, about six, holding the handle)
(Lily gives him a little wave)
(the boy doesn't wave back; he doesn't smile; he just looks at her)
Lily (V.O.): Okay. Tough crowd.

[SCENE 2 — Target, the chip aisle, a few minutes later]
(Lily comes around the end of the aisle and almost runs her basket into his cart)
Man: Oh. Hey. Sorry.
Lily: No, my fault.
Man: We keep doing this.
(he laughs and rubs the back of his neck)
Man: Okay, I'm just gonna say it. You're really cute. That's, sorry, that's a weird thing to say in the chip aisle.
Lily: No, it's, thank you.
(she's playing with her keys while she talks; the little alarm swings off her finger)
Man: Please tell me your boyfriend's not about to come around that corner.
Lily: No. It's just me and a box of candles.
Man: Good. I mean, not good. Okay, I'm really bad at this.
(she laughs)
Man: What are the candles for?
Lily: My little brother's birthday. He's turning ten. I'm on candle duty.
Man: Big job.
Lily: Huge.
(he nods at her keys)
Man: Is that one of those alarm things?
Lily: Oh. Yeah. My mom got it for me when I left for school. I mostly just like the color.
Man: It's a good color. You know those don't really work, right? Nobody even looks up anymore.
(he says it like a joke)
Lily: Wow. Okay. Thank you.
Man: No, I'm sorry, that's such a dad thing to say. We've been in here since like noon, and he can't pick a cake. I'm losing my mind.
Man: Can I get your Instagram? Is that, is that what people do now?
(Lily laughs and holds her hand out for his phone)
(she types in her name and hands it back)
Man: Lily. Okay. I'll see you around, Lily.
(she walks off and looks back once; he's still smiling)
(she smiles at the floor the whole way to the end of the aisle)

[SCENE 3 — the kitchen at home, the same minute]
(Jen frosts a sheet cake with a big blue 10 on it; balloons are tied to the chairs)
(her phone is on speaker on the counter)
Jen: Hey, are you checking out? Everybody's here at six and I have zero candles.
Lily (on the phone): I'm walking to the register right now. Also, um, I met a guy.
Jen: In Target?
Lily (on the phone): Mom, he's so cute. Like, unfairly cute.
Jen: Okay, well, be careful. He could be a kidnapper.
Lily (on the phone): Mom, he's way too hot to abduct people.
(Jen laughs)
Jen: Candles. Six o'clock. I love you.
(she hangs up, still smiling, and licks frosting off her thumb)

[SCENE 4 — Target, the front doors, the sun going down]
(Lily walks out with her bag, smiling at her phone)
(on the screen: a new follower)

[SCENE 5 — the parking lot, her car in the back row]
(a gray car is parked right next to hers now, with the hood up)
(the man is bent over the engine with his sleeves rolled up)
(he looks up and smiles)
Man: Okay, I swear this is not on purpose.
Lily: Are you following me?
Man: Battery's dead. Of course it is.
(she laughs)
(she looks at the gray car)
(a flash: the same spot, empty, when she pulled in an hour ago)
Lily (V.O.): That spot was empty when I got here.
(a flash: the chip aisle, the man laughing, "I've been in here since like noon")
Lily (V.O.): He said he's been here since noon.
(she looks back at him; he's still smiling at her)
Man: Hey, can I ask you a huge favor? My jumper cables are in a bag on the passenger seat. Could you grab them for me? My hands are disgusting.
(he holds up his hands, black with grease, and wipes them on a white rag)
(she looks at the passenger door)
(she looks through the back window: a car seat, empty)
(she looks around the lot; there's no little boy anywhere)
Lily (V.O.): Where's the kid?
Lily: You know what, I'm so sorry, my mom's waiting on these candles. I should really get going.
Man: Totally. Yeah, no, go.
(he smiles, and his eyes go past her to the store doors)

[SCENE 6 — her car, through her eyes]
(her hand pulls the driver's door open)
(she drops into the seat and tosses the bag on the passenger side)
(her keys are shaking; she can't find the right one)
(in the side mirror, the man stands up from the hood)
(he's walking fast)
(he's running)
(the white rag is still in his hand)
(her thumb finds the little mint-green alarm)
(cut to black)

[SCENE 7 — the hospital room, night]
(a heart monitor beeps; Lily's eyes open)
(the Target bag on the chair, the candles sticking out of it)
Jen: Oh, baby. Hi. Hi. You're okay.
(Jen holds Lily's face in both hands and cries)
Lily: Mom, the candles.
(Jen laughs through it)
Jen: Nobody cares about the candles.
(the detective knocks on the open door)
Detective: Lily? I'm Detective Hale. Mind if I sit?
Lily: Where is he?
Detective: The man you met at Target today. He wasn't there to shop. He was there to pick a girl to abduct tonight.
Lily: But he was so nice.
Detective: They're always nice. That's the whole job.
Lily: He had a little boy with him.
Detective: That boy isn't his. He was waiting in the other car the whole time.
Lily: What other car?
(he turns his laptop around: the parking lot camera, no sound)
(on the screen, the gray car pulls into the empty spot next to Lily's car)
Detective: That gray car was stolen two days ago. He moved it next to yours while you were checking out.
Detective: The hood, the dead battery, the cables on the passenger seat. He needed you leaning into that car with your back to him.
(on the screen, Lily walks to her own door instead)
(the man runs at her)
(her hand comes up)
(the strobe flashes white across the lot)
(every head in the lot turns at the same time)
(a man drops his grocery bags; a woman at the cart return points)
(the man backs away from Lily's door, walks two rows over, and gets into a silver minivan)
(the minivan pulls out slow, like nothing happened)
(Jen covers her mouth)
Detective: There was chloroform on that rag. You got enough of it to pass out in your seat, but you pulled that pin first, and he didn't get you out of that car.
Detective: Look. He told you those alarms don't work, right?
(Lily nods)
Detective: He says that to every girl. He needs you to believe it, because he needs it quiet and he needs nobody looking. The second forty people looked up, he was done.
Detective: He spent all day in that store picking. He's not picking the girl who'd fight him. He's picking the one who'll be polite to him.
Detective: And the account that followed you? He made it three days ago.
(Lily looks at the ceiling)
Jen: She called me from the store. She told me he was cute, and I made a joke about it. I told her he could be a kidnapper, and we both laughed.
(Jen can't finish)
Jen: We live in a nice town.
Detective: So did the last two girls.
Jen: Are they okay?
Detective: One of them is.
(he looks at his hands)
Detective: Ma'am, he doesn't look like what you're picturing. That's the point. It's not a guy in a van. It's a nice-looking dad with a little boy, in a Target, in your town, on a Saturday afternoon.
Detective: Thank god she had that thing on her keys. Most of the girls in my files didn't, and some of them I never got back.
Detective: Where'd you get it?
Jen: It's, um, Palace Guard. I got it when she left for school, and she picked the color.
(he nods)
Detective: Good. You made the right choice. My own girls carry the same one.
Detective: I've had the cheap ones handed to me in evidence bags. Dead battery, or cracked the first time they hit the ground.
Jen: I picked it because it's a real 130 decibels and it has the strobe light.
Detective: That light's in the footage. You can see it from the cart return.
(on the tray by the bed, Lily's keys; the little mint-green alarm, the pin gone)
Lily: He told me it was a good color.
(cut to black)

[SCENE 8 — close: the alarm on Lily's keys on the kitchen counter, next to the uncut birthday cake; one on Jen's purse; one on a backpack by the door]
Jen (V.O.): My daughter called me from Target to tell me she met a cute guy, and I made a joke about it.
Jen (V.O.): So maybe your daughter's smart about people. Mine is. He was nicer to her than most boys have ever been.
Jen (V.O.): Maybe she'd notice something off. She noticed the little boy didn't smile, and she still gave him her Instagram.
Jen (V.O.): Maybe you live in a nice town. So do we. He picked our Target on a Saturday afternoon, with a parking lot full of people.
Jen (V.O.): Maybe you're her grandma, and you worry about her more than anybody knows. You can't be in that parking lot with her. Nobody can.
Jen (V.O.): And maybe she's heard those alarms don't work. He's the one who told her that.
(close: the alarm on a key ring next to a car key; a thumb pulls the pin; the strobe fires)
Jen (V.O.): It clips on her keys, it's lighter than a car key, and it's as loud as an ambulance. It won't go off in her purse, and it's not a weapon, so nobody can use it on her.
Jen (V.O.): I bought three and got two free, so it came out to less than twenty dollars each. There's a thirty-day guarantee too, I think.
Jen (V.O.): A detective gal makes them in small batches, and they sell out. So if the link still works, they've still got some.
Jen (V.O.): That detective has sat with mothers whose daughters met a nice guy too, and didn't have anything on their keys. I almost didn't buy it.
Jen (V.O.): Share this with every mother you know so their daughter doesn't get abducted or assaulted.
Jen (V.O.): The link's right there. Don't wait until it's too late.
