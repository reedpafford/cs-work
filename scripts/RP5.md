# RP5 — Three men at Target (the movie)
Persona: daughter (narrator) · Awareness: UA · Format: Movie
Status: drafting
Parent: — · Lever: —
Cast: Paige (16, tallest girl in her grade, narrates) · Dana (her mom, 40s) · Paige's brother (older) · the three men (gray sweatsuits, work boots, no lines) · the store manager (red shirt) · the cart kid · the police officer · the detective (50s, flat and quiet) · two teammates

## V1
### Text Hook
Stay alert, parents. Predators have a new way of abducting girls at Target.
### Script
[SCENE 1 — Target parking lot, just after sunset, the back half under a light that's half out]
(a white van with no windows rolls up three spaces from a tall girl in a volleyball sweatshirt)
Paige (V.O.): Three predators tried to abduct me in a Target parking lot. I'm the girl who broke my brother's nose, and I couldn't even scream.
(the side door is already open)
(two men in gray sweatsuits get out and walk straight at her)
(her mouth opens and nothing comes out)
(cut to black)

[SCENE 2 — the family living room, two years earlier]
(Paige at fourteen and her older brother fight over the remote on the couch)
(he grabs her arm; she swings once; he's on the floor holding his nose)
Dana (O.S.): Paige!
Paige (V.O.): Okay, so you need to know who I am first. I'm the tallest girl in my grade, I'm the loud one at dinner, and when I was fourteen I broke my brother's nose over the remote.
Paige (V.O.): If you asked my mom which of her kids would be fine in a bad situation, she'd say me before you finished the question.

[SCENE 3 — the driveway, August, the week Paige got her license]
(Dana clips a small black alarm onto Paige's car keys)
Dana: Just leave it on there, okay?
Paige: What if it goes off in the middle of class?
Dana: It's got an anti-misfire thing, so it won't. And if it ever does, the pin goes right back in.
(Paige rolls her eyes and drops the keys in her bag)
Paige (V.O.): It was like the phone charger in the glovebox. You don't think about it.

[SCENE 4 — the school lot after volleyball practice, just after six]
(Paige walks out with her knee pads around her ankles and checks her phone)
(a text from Mom: can you stop at target and grab milk and eggs)
(she types back: k)
(at home, Dana flips on the porch light)
Paige (V.O.): It was a Tuesday, just after sunset. Mom remembers because she'd just turned the porch light on.

[SCENE 5 — inside Target, 6:40]
(Paige takes a carton of eggs off the shelf)
(at the end of the aisle stands a man in a gray sweatsuit and work boots, with no cart and his hands in his pockets)
(he looks at her, then looks away too fast)
Paige (V.O.): Weird. But whatever.
(in the next aisle, another one: same sweatsuit, same boots, nothing in his hands)
(by the freezers, a third)
Paige (V.O.): I figured they were, like, a construction crew on a break, three guys from the same job grabbing a drink.
(she turns down the cereal aisle; one of them walks past the far end of it)
(she turns down the next one; another walks past the end of that one too)
Paige (V.O.): But every aisle I turned down, one of them walked past the end of it. Not toward me, just past, like they were keeping track of where I was.
(she stops and looks down at the milk and eggs in her basket)
Paige (V.O.): Three grown men dressed the same, in a Target at 6:40 on a Tuesday, and not one of them was buying anything.

[SCENE 6 — self-checkout]
(a manager in a red shirt stands ten feet away, reading a clipboard)
(Paige looks at her)
(Paige opens her mouth, then closes it)
(she scans her eggs; the manager never looks up)

[SCENE 7 — the kitchen at home, the same minute]
(Dana checks her phone: Paige's dot is sitting in the Target parking lot)
(she puts the phone face down on the counter and turns the oven on)
Paige (V.O.): My mom checked my dot. It was sitting in the Target parking lot, right where it was supposed to be. So she put her phone down and preheated the oven.

[SCENE 8 — the parking lot, the back half, the light that's half out]
(Paige walks to her car with her keys in one hand and the bag in the other)
(three steps from her door, the white van rolls up and stops three spaces away)
(the side door is already open)
(two of the men get out and walk straight at her, not fast, just straight)
(she opens her mouth and nothing comes out)
Paige (V.O.): I opened my mouth to scream and nothing came out. It felt like my throat was glued shut.
(her thumb finds the little alarm on her keys)
(the eggs hit the asphalt)
(she pulls the pin)
(the siren screams across the lot and the strobe flashes white on the van)
(a man loading his trunk two rows over turns around)
(the kid pushing a line of carts turns around)
(a woman with a stroller stops so fast the stroller rolls a foot ahead of her)
(the two men stop)
(they look at each other)
(they turn and walk back to the van like they forgot something)
(the van pulls out with the side door still open)
(the cart kid lets go of his carts and runs after it with his phone up)
Cart Kid: I got the plate! I got it!
(Paige gets in her car, locks the doors, and holds her keys against her chest)

[SCENE 9 — the kitchen at home]
(the oven beeps)
(Dana's phone buzzes on the counter: Paige)
Dana: Hey, did you get the eggs?
(she can't make out what Paige is saying)
Dana: Baby, slow down. I can't understand you.
Officer (on the phone): Ma'am, this is the police. Your daughter's safe, she's with me. Three men just tried to abduct her in the Target parking lot.
(Dana slides down the cabinet to the kitchen floor)
Dana: I'm coming. Tell her I'm coming.
(she runs out the front door in her slippers)

[SCENE 10 — the Target parking lot, twenty minutes later, police lights]
(a police officer stands at Paige's window)
(Dana runs across the lot in her slippers)
(there's egg all over the asphalt by the back tire)
(Paige unlocks the door; her keys are still in her fist and she won't let go of them)
Dana: Let me see you. Let me see you.
(the manager in the red shirt walks up to the officer)
Manager: We've had complaints about those three for two weeks.
Dana: Two weeks?
(the manager looks at the asphalt)
Dana: And nobody said a word to a sixteen-year-old buying eggs.
(Dana turns back to Paige)
Dana: She was right there, honey. Why didn't you say something to her?
(Paige looks at the floor of the car)
Paige: Mom, I didn't want to be dramatic.
(Dana closes her eyes)
Paige (V.O.): My mom says that one's on her. She raised me to be polite. She raised me not to make a fuss in a store.

[SCENE 11 — the kitchen table, two days later]
(the detective sits in Dad's chair; he hasn't touched the coffee Dana made him)
(Paige sits on the stairs, where they can't see her)
Detective: The plate on that van matched two other reports this same week. The shopping center across the street.
Dana: Two other girls?
(he nods)
Dana: Are they okay?
Detective: One of them is.
Dana: And the other one?
(he looks at his hands)
Detective: I can't talk about her.
Paige (V.O.): I think about that girl every day. My mom thinks about her mother.
(he turns his laptop around: the parking lot camera, no sound)
(on the screen, Paige's hand comes down and every head in the lot turns at the same time)
Paige (V.O.): There's no sound on the footage. My mom's watched it eleven times. I've never watched it.
Dana: She broke her brother's nose. Why couldn't she just scream?
Detective: Look. Why do you think they wore the same clothes and didn't carry anything? Why do you think the van had no windows, and the door was open before they ever got out?
(Dana doesn't answer)
Detective: Because men like that plan everything around one thing. They need it quiet, and they need nobody watching.
Detective: She couldn't have fought off two grown men, and she didn't need to. The only thing that really stops them is their own decision to walk away, and she made that decision for them the second forty people looked up.
Detective: Noise is predator repellent. They know it better than anybody.
(on the stairs, Paige pulls her knees up to her chest)
Detective: And freezing isn't weakness. It happens to almost every girl, the tough ones too. The ones I never get back are the ones who froze and nobody heard them.
Detective: I'll never tell a parent an alarm is a force field. But drawing attention is the most powerful thing she has, and it's the one thing they can't take off her.
Detective: What kind is it?
Dana: I, um, I don't know.
(she opens her laptop and scrolls back to an order from August)
Dana: Palace Guard.
(he nods)
Detective: Good.
Detective: I've had the cheap colorful ones handed to me in evidence bags. Dead battery, or cracked the first time they hit the ground. Some girls carried them for years, and they didn't work the one night it mattered.
Detective: My own daughters carry the same one she does.
Dana: I almost didn't buy it. I picked that one because it's a real 130 decibels, it's got the strobe light, and it was the only one she'd agree to carry.
Paige: Because it doesn't go off in class.
(they both turn; she's been on the stairs the whole time)
Detective: That's probably the only reason it was on her keys that night instead of in a drawer.
Dana: And it turns out it saved her life.

[SCENE 12 — the weeks after, mostly silent]
(Paige's bedroom light is on at two in the morning)
(the next night, it's on again)
Paige (V.O.): I slept with my light on for a week.
(the next Monday, she walks back into volleyball practice with the alarm clipped to her bag)
Paige (V.O.): I wasn't going to let three men in sweatsuits take that from me too.
(three weeks later: the gym lot after practice, dark)
(two guys standing by a truck at the edge of the lot look over at her)
(her hand goes straight to her keys, like checking for a seatbelt)
(Dana sees it from the car)
Paige (V.O.): Nobody told me to do that.
(two teammates lean in the car window)
Teammate: Wait, where'd you get that?
(Dana is already on her phone, ordering five more)
Paige (V.O.): Mom ordered five more before I even finished telling her.

[SCENE 13 — close: the alarm on Paige's keys on the kitchen counter; one on Dana's purse; one on her little sister's backpack]
Paige (V.O.): And we live in a safe neighborhood. We leave the garage open. My mom never believed this kind of thing actually happens, let alone to me, in a Target, with a manager ten feet away.
Paige (V.O.): So maybe your daughter's tough like me. I broke my brother's nose, and my body still wouldn't move.
Paige (V.O.): Maybe she'd ask somebody for help. I walked right past a manager because I didn't want to be dramatic.
Paige (V.O.): Maybe you track her phone. So does my mom. The dot showed her exactly where I was while two men walked toward me.
Paige (V.O.): Maybe you're her grandma, and you worry about her more than anybody knows. You can't be in that parking lot with her. Nobody can.
Paige (V.O.): The only difference between me and the girl the detective couldn't talk about was what was on my keys.
(close: the alarm on a key ring next to a car key; a thumb pulls the pin; the strobe fires)
Paige (V.O.): It clips on your keys, it's lighter than a car key, and it's as loud as an ambulance. It's not a weapon, so a school can't take it and nobody can use it on her.
Paige (V.O.): My mom bought three and got two free, so it came out to less than twenty dollars each. There's a thirty-day guarantee too. She checked, because she reads everything now.
Paige (V.O.): A detective gal makes them in small batches, and we waited about a week for ours because they were sold out. So if the link still works, they've still got some.
Paige (V.O.): That detective has sat with parents whose girls froze just like I did, and nobody heard them. And my mom almost didn't buy it.
Paige (V.O.): Share this with every mother you know so their daughter doesn't get abducted or assaulted.
Paige (V.O.): The link's right there. Don't wait until it's too late.
