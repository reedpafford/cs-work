## THE FIVE PILLARS

This is the framework. Specific formats win for a few months and then saturate — the pillars don't change.

**1. Avatar & Environment** — a character and world that are unmistakably yours, consistent every video.

**2. Content Concepts & Scripts** — ideas nobody else is running. This is the pillar with the most room in it.

**3. Editing Style** — your own visual language, not the default everyone uses.

**4. Realism** — it doesn't read as AI.

**5. Volume & Consistency** — output, sustained.

**Hit all five and you generally go viral. Where you're not innovating in one, you have to make up for it in the others.**

### The thing to understand about formats

**Innovating inside any pillar gets rewarded.** Whoever does it first wins, everyone copies it, it becomes baseline, and the edge moves somewhere else.

The three-second visual hook opening is a good example — it was an innovation inside Content Concepts, it worked enormously well, and now most of the network opens that way. It's still a solid format. It's no longer an edge.

**So don't treat any current winning style as the definition of good content.** Treat it as evidence that innovating in that pillar pays. When I bring you an idea, the useful question is *which pillar am I innovating in, and is it actually new* — not *does this match the format that's working right now*.

### What's currently saturated

- Talking head plus subtitle overlay as the whole edit
- Generic wellness avatars in generic kitchens
- Formats copied from the network without meaningful change

### What breaks realism

Five things that make content read as AI instantly: orange or cartoony skin · green-screen or floating backgrounds · face lighting that doesn't match the room · hands · robotic or inconsistent voice.

### Three levels of research

- **Level 1** — find winning Jupi content, improve it by 20%
- **Level 2** — find winners for other gut products, adapt them
- **Level 3** — find viral content outside the niche entirely, build something new from it

Level 1 forever caps you out. Level 3 is where new formats get invented.

## THE ICP LIBRARY

Eleven distinct buyers. An avatar should have **one** as its primary lane — crossover into adjacent ones happens naturally, the point is a clear home base rather than speaking to everyone.

### Female

**F1. The Menopause-Era Woman (45–60)** — largest and most underserved
Bloats from foods that never bothered her, weight settling around the middle, run down. Declining estrogen slows gut motility and shifts the microbiome. Feels dismissed ("it's just your age") and betrayed by her own body.

**F2. The Stressed Career Woman / Caregiver (35–55)**
Stomach in knots, stress bloat even when barely eating, gets sick the moment she rests. Chronic cortisol keeps the body in fight-or-flight, slowing digestion and suppressing immunity. Exhausted, feels punished for working hard.

**F3. The Always-Sick Woman (40–65)**
Catches everything, two weeks to recover, does everything right and still gets ill. Much of immune activity is gut-based. Frustrated, quietly afraid of becoming fragile.

**F4. The Sensitive-Gut Woman (30–55)**
Mental list of trigger foods, scans menus, plans life around her stomach. Microbiome imbalance plus heightened sensitivity; restrictive eating narrows the diet and worsens it. Social embarrassment, wants food freedom back.

**F5. The Depleted-Gut Woman (35–60)**
"My stomach hasn't been right since those antibiotics." Beneficial bacteria cleared, microbiome takes months to rebuild. Stuck between necessary medicine and how it left her.

**F6. The Desk-Life Woman (35–55)**
The 5pm bloat, unbuttoning jeans by evening, "I eat a salad and still bloat." Sitting slows motility, rushed desk lunches impair digestion. Wants a low-effort fix, sceptical of overhauls.

**F7. The Run-Down Mum (30–48)**
Catches every bug the kids bring home, twice as bad. Eats standing up or not at all. Won't buy for herself — will buy to stay strong for her family. September and January are peak months.

### Male — all four lanes are wide open

**M1. The Bloated Midlife Man (45–65)**
The belly that isn't just beer. Heavy, distended, sluggish after meals, doesn't talk about it. Won't engage with anything that feels feminine or medical. His wife (F1–F3) is often the actual buyer.

**M2. The Stressed Working Man (35–55)**
Gut acts up under pressure, run down, first to catch anything. Ignores symptoms until they're loud. Wants to feel strong and dependable — performance framing, never vulnerability.

**M3. The Older Man Slowing Down (55–70)**
Always run down, slow to bounce back, digestion not what it was. Declining enzyme and stomach acid production. Wants to stay capable and independent.

**M4. The Fit-But-Bloated Man (30–48)**
Trains hard, eats clean, still bloated. High-protein diets are hard on digestion, low food variety, overtraining suppresses gut function. Treats his body as a project.

### The rule

A demographic is not an ICP. "Women 35+" is a demographic. "A 47-year-old who unbuttons her jeans at her desk by 5pm and has been told twice it's just stress" is an ICP. You can write ten scripts for the second and none for the first.

**Niching harder inside a lane usually makes an avatar stronger**, because nothing in the content is wasted.

## SYMPTOM & CAUSE LIBRARY

Raw material for hooks. Each is a hidden truth that makes someone stop scrolling because you finally explained why they feel the way they feel.

**Hormonal** — perimenopause slowing gut motility · progesterone drop stalling digestion · high cortisol shutting digestion down · PMS fluid retention · thyroid dysfunction (hugely overlooked in women 35+) · insulin resistance · oestrogen dominance · birth control altering the microbiome and depleting B vitamins, zinc, magnesium · coming off the pill taking 6–18 months to re-regulate

**Gut microbiome** — dysbiosis · SIBO · candida overgrowth · leaky gut · post-antibiotic collapse · low stomach acid (very common over 40) · declining digestive enzymes · low bile production causing bloating after fatty meals

**Lifestyle & diet** — eating too fast · distracted desk eating · ultra-processed emulsifiers damaging the gut lining · artificial sweeteners feeding the wrong bacteria · high-FODMAP without knowing it · gluten and dairy sensitivity · too much fibre too fast · not enough water · coffee on an empty stomach

**The wellness paradox — healthy habits causing the problem** — over-supplementing · protein powder with sweeteners and thickeners · probiotic rebound · greens powder inulin · intermittent fasting slowing the migrating motor complex · overtraining suppressing gut immunity · too clean a diet (the gut needs 30 plant foods a week) · cold water during meals

**Hydration-specific** — water without electrolytes meaning chronic under-hydration while drinking 2L a day · overhydration flushing sodium · morning coffee before water · alcohol disrupting gut hydration for 3–4 days · sparkling water adding CO2 · sweat electrolytes never replaced

**Physical & structural** — weak pelvic floor · visceral hypersensitivity · fibroids · hiatus hernia · slow colonic transit

**Nervous system** — gut-brain axis dysregulation · trauma held in the body · poor sleep altering gut bacteria overnight · burnout

**Lesser-known** — coeliac (widely undiagnosed) · bowel endometriosis (commonly first told it's IBS) · gastroparesis · MCAS · histamine intolerance · oxalate sensitivity · bile acid malabsorption

**Skin, energy & immunity crossover** — gut-skin axis driving acne and rosacea · nutrient malabsorption despite eating perfectly · a large share of immune activity being gut-based · chronic low-grade inflammation causing brain fog and afternoon crashes
