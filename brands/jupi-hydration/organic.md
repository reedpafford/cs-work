# Jupi Hydration — how organic scripts work
> **Jupi only.** Never apply this to another brand. Source: the copywriting masterclass with Tankas, hosted by Ollie (Kinveo, 2026-11-19), and Reed's calls (2026-11-23). Organic AI-avatar posts on Facebook and Instagram that end on an Amazon link, not paid ads. Where this file and the house rules in CLAUDE.md disagree on how a Jupi script is shaped, this file wins. The word rule, the image rule and Reed's edits still win over everything.

## Who's watching
- **She came to relax, not to think.** People in their 40s and 50s open Instagram and Facebook to wind down after a long day. So the job is to pack in as much information as possible in the easiest possible way to take in.
- **Nine-year-old words.** Even easier than the house's sixth grade. "A fire going on inside your belly," not "gut inflammation." If a line comes out too simple, toughen it up one notch from there, never the other way round.
- **US English, US references** (Reed, 2026-11-23). Mom, fiber, eight glasses, a gallon. Never mum, fibre or litres.
- **The kind of content she watches is part of the buyer.** It isn't only "the bloated 45-year-old." It's the bloated 45-year-old who watches recipe videos, or workout videos, or Costco hauls. Pick both halves.

## What makes a script good
- **Find an idea by its virality.** An idea that gets more likes than its usual baseline has a working skeleton. Keep the skeleton and make it more interesting, or broader, or narrower.
- **Break the inspiration down line by line before writing.** For each line: what does it do in her head? Then rewrite the parts that do the work (the 80/20). Rewording lines that do nothing is just copying with extra steps. Never copy the words outright, because Meta catches copied scripts and they don't get the reach.
- **The product has to really solve the problem the script sets up.** A gut-flush script with Jupi pasted in at the bottom doesn't make sense. Jupi helps with bloating its own way: ginger, enzymes, electrolytes. If commenters or Amazon reviewers say it didn't do what the video said, trust dies.
- **Use her words.** Reddit threads, the long comments under viral videos where people describe their problem, and Amazon reviews. A 22-year-old who writes from his own head will never sound like a 45-year-old woman. A line that sounds wrong to us can be exactly how she talks.
- **Somewhere in the script, hit all of these:** her desire, her problem, her behavior (what she actually does, which isn't always what she believes) and her emotion. Open on whichever one she relates to most: the dream state, the pain, the emotion or the behavior. There's no one best framework.
- **Problem and solution is the backbone,** but a problem followed by a solution isn't enough on its own. Her emotions and behaviors go in before the solution.

## The shape that's winning: the recipe script (Serene's socks script, the network's most viral)
1. **Call her out on her symptom and give the cause straight away,** in the first three lines. (The other way is Dr. Berg style: hold back the answer, "the number one fruit for…", and drag it to the end. Both work, for different viewers.)
2. **Authority in one line:** "I've been telling people this for decades." Keep the claims believable. If a claim is too big, no one believes it, even from a doctor.
3. **The root cause again, and what it does to her body,** with the symptoms she has: bloating after every meal, tired all the time.
4. **The fixes that didn't work:** lemon water, apple cider vinegar.
5. **The recipe,** with the product as one ingredient in it.
6. **The mechanism:** what the product does inside her so she gets the result.
7. **The benefit.**
8. **Proof and the dream state, once:** "people in their forties, fifties and beyond… tell me their bloating goes away."
9. **The CTA.**

What the socks script got wrong, so ours don't:
- **Name the key thing plainly.** It never clearly said soursop was the reason it works, and people scrolling are half asleep, so they won't work it out. For Jupi, say what the key thing is out loud.
- **Proof once.** Its second "my clients tell me" was dead weight.
- **Once she's sold, stop.** Bringing up the failed fixes again after the pitch is out of place.
- **Cut filler** ("that's why I never go without it" didn't carry anything).

## Where the product goes
- **Late, and on the side.** The video is about the recipe, or the comparison, or the routine. Jupi is one ingredient or one product among the others. Viewers drop off whenever a product appears; that can't be avoided.
- **The desire is built in the body of the script, not the CTA.** If the script makes her want it enough, she buys without being pushed. A good bridge keeps the CTA from sounding out of place.
- **The CTA that has worked since April 2025 (Rosabella, TikTok Shop) and still works:** most brands fill theirs with cheap fillers and chemicals, which is why the only one I trust is Jupi. A long avatar CTA, these days arriving through the recipe.

## The CTA on organic (Reed, 2026-11-23, JRP1): always two parts, different from paid
1. **Where to buy, said out loud, at the product intro or the close.** Amazon by name, the search term, and the profile link. Reed's example: "but you can find it on Amazon by searching Jupi Hydration. Or to make it easier, just click the link in my profile." There's no ad button on an organic post, so the script has to tell her where it is.
2. **Engagement asks at the very end, to push reach.** A share, a comment keyword with a reward, and the follow so the DM lands. Reed's example: "Send this to someone who needs to see it. And if you comment the word glow, I'll send you the morning routine I take with this. Just be sure you follow me first so my message delivers." The keyword and the reward change to fit each video (an example, not a line to reuse).

## Formats now (2026-11), with where Jupi fits
- **Recipe:** the most effective for Jupi. She's already trying recipes to fix her symptoms, so a little education gets her to try a new one.
- **Product comparison** (Costco: walk the aisle, say what each thing costs and what it's good for): converts well even on low views, because she's already in shopping mode and writing down what to buy. For Jupi it's weaker than the recipe, because a consumable has fewer rivals than makeup does. Middle of funnel. Untested ideas: "cheap stuff from Amazon that helps your health" (better conversion, less to look at); the in-store stack, "this does that, but take it with this and you get…".
- **The ingredient stack** (from the magnesium glycinate sleep post): "if you take X you get this, take X with Y you get more, take X with Y and Z…", then Jupi at the end as the one that has them all, then its key benefits, then proof, carried through her emotions, behaviors, pains and desires. Tankas would put Jupi at the end, not the start, but both are worth testing. Every ingredient claim has to be true.
- **Celebrity dupes** (JRP2, from the Serene Herbs soursop "facelift" post): celebrity bodies as the anchor, then expensive fix → cheap dupe, then "the one thing I don't dupe" is Jupi. **Every dupe is a brand name or a household item she already owns or has seen in the aisle, on both sides** (La Mer → Nivea, the blue tin). That recognition is why she keeps listening. Never a generic activity or a no-name product ("a walk after dinner," "peppermint tea") (Reed, 2026-11-23) **And every dupe is the same type of product, the whole way through, and Jupi is that type too:** all skincare in the swipe, all supplements for Jupi (fat burners, debloaters, gut pills). Never shapewear, then a spice, then a pill (Reed, 2026-11-23). **The store the script names is the set:** say Walmart and the video is shot in a Walmart aisle, and every cheap dupe comes off that store's shelf, so name one store, never Walmart in one line and CVS in the next (Reed, JRP2). **The product comes in as a kind of thing first and the brand name second:** the swipe says "soursop bitters" and only names Serene Herbs at "the only brand I trust." Reed's JRP2 shape: he *recommends* the kind ("an anti-bloat mix that puts the turmeric with black pepper"), lists what the best ones also have ("the best mixes also have ginger, and enzymes…"), then "there's only one mix that combines all of these, and isn't made with cheap fillers and sugar, and it's called Jupi Hydration." Naming Jupi in the mechanism makes the trust line pointless (Reed, JRP2).
- **Wide open:** recipe swapped for a workout routine (a different audience) · conspiracy mixed with recipe · sleep.
- **Open angles (Ollie):** hydration and immunity. Nobody is pushing them. Rich's "stop drinking plain water, it isn't hydrating you" built on the recipe skeleton is doing well because it's in its own lane.
- **Angles come from the reviews.** If Amazon reviewers say they have more energy and sleep better because they feel hydrated, that's an angle.

## Top and middle of funnel
- Post both. They're nearly the same script. **Top of funnel** (she doesn't know she has the problem) adds a couple of lines at the start that teach her she has it. **Middle of funnel** (she knows) cuts those lines, because she won't sit through being told something she already knows, and a top-of-funnel viewer won't sit through a fix for a problem she doesn't know she has.
- Each post is its own lane to its own audience. Never count on someone seeing one post and then the next.

## AI tells (Tankas: be different from AI, not better than it)
- **Symptom bundles.** AI writes three symptoms to a sentence, every time. Give one, or two, or four, and let them sit in different sentences.
- AI talks like a data center, all facts and structure. People can feel it, and Meta favors posts that feel original. So I don't write whole Jupi scripts. I work line by line: simplify the words, offer options, and lift her language from the research. Reed writes. (This matches the house rule.)
- Fresh eyes: reread a draft a day later before calling it done.

## Reed's calls (2026-11-23)
- Don't overcomplicate the message. A line doesn't have to be something only Jupi could say. The hydration and immunity angles can be broad as long as the product really delivers.
- No disclosure or personal-attributes notes on organic posts. Don't flag them.
