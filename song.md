# song.md — song ads (draft until Reed runs one)

A song ad is a normal spoken script that gets sung. No rhyme, no chorus, no meter. Reed (2026-11-09): "Don't think too much about it being a song." Everything in CLAUDE.md still holds: the word rule, the persona's register, the regret beat, the share CTA, "Don't wait." What's different is how the story gets told, and that comes from the winners below.

**Source:** 12 competitor song ads Reed shared on 2026-11-09 (supplements, mushroom coffee, a dog probiotic), all 3D animated with word-by-word captions, 2 to 9 minutes long. Transcripts are in `archive/song-ads/`. Whisper mangles some sung words, but the structure reads clean. The strongest: 02 (oregano, husband sends photos to his ex-wife; it ran in two cuts), 05 (cowboy brother narrates), 08 (sister from Okinawa), 09 to 11 (the dog sings to its owner).

## The skeleton (nearly every winner)
1. **Hook = the worst moment, said as an action, with ages or a number.** "My husband took a picture of my ass… and sent it to his ex-wife. She's 48, I'm 35." (On RP3 Reed kept JC31's own "Stay alert, parents" hook and its "watch to the end" line, so a proven script's opening stays as it is.)
2. **"So here's what happened" / "Let me back up."** Then **who the person is, with a trait the peak is about to break.** Wade is "the biggest mouth in the county" and gets knocked out cold. Paige is "the loudest one at dinner" and can't scream.
3. **The scene, one small action per line, in present tense once it gets tense.** "The girls went to the ladies' room. Two came back." Never summarize the scary stretch. Specific clock, place and object: 2am, the pantry hallway, a hot dish in her hands.
4. **No stepping out of the scene until it's over.** A line spoken straight to the viewer mid-scene breaks the tension; it goes in the "Maybe" lines instead.
5. **Dramatic irony before the peak.** The narrator shows where she was while it happened ("And me? I'm at home, looking at her little dot"). The viewer knows and she doesn't.
6. **The private low point.** 3am, the mirror, throwing up at 5am. For us: she slept in my bed, I sent her for eggs.
7. **A trusted person explains, in quoted dialogue, and takes the blame off her.** Mama, a sister flown in, a coworker, the old dog at the park. "You don't have a weight problem, you have a drainage problem." Ours: the detective at the kitchen table, "that's not you being weak… they count on that." The mechanism lives in his mouth, never as stats to camera.
8. **Proof over time, through other people's reactions.** Week one, week two, a coworker asks, a brother texts. The narrator never says "it works."
9. **The payoff in the same place as the wound**, and it calls back to the hook. "I ran out of eggs again. Paige had her keys before I could even ask." The winners' last line is a callback ("the photos look different now"); ours puts the callback before the regret beat, because the script still ends on regret, the share CTA and "Don't wait."
10. **"Maybe…" lines aimed at the viewer, built from this story's details**, so she checks her own daughter the way the health ads make you check your own body. "Maybe she's the one you never worry about." · "And maybe you think, my girl would scream. So did I."
11. **A short product section after the story**, then "if you still see the link below, they're still in stock."

## Perspective
One narrator for the whole song. Either the person it happened to, or someone one step away who saw it (the brother, the stepdaughter, the security guard). For us: **the mother narrates, and the screen shows the daughter** (Reed, RP3). She knows every detail because "she told me everything later" and she has watched the footage.
The dog ads have the one who uses the product singing to the one who buys it ("Mom stop ignoring my scratching" · "You watched me struggle and you looked away" · "She's been waiting on you"). That's daughter to mother for us. Untested; it can't imply she didn't make it home unless it stays implied under the word rule.

## Mood (a note for whoever makes the music, in chat, never in the script file)
Dark true-crime score, minor key, not horror effects. Her voice: in her 50s, close to the mic, unpolished; the music carries the fear. Per section: hook sparse, almost spoken · the setup warm · the stalking a low pulse that builds, with one beat of false relief · the freeze near silence · the pull the hit, like a siren · the low point one instrument, her voice cracking · the authority low and steady · proof over time lifts · the payoff warm · "Maybe" and the product plain, so every detail comes through · the regret beat voice only · the share CTA and "Don't wait" spoken over the last chord.
Reed (RP3): not country. The story is suspense, so it should sound like it.

## Worked example
RP3 (JC31 as a song): `scripts/RP3.md`. JC31's dialogue word for word, about 1,000 words, 5 to 6 minutes sung. No text hook (Reed). Three variations with the same script; only the music and the scenes change (moody and gloomy, etc.). That tests the treatment, not the words.

## What I do on "make this a song"
Use the source script's dialogue as is, hook and retention line included (Reed, RP3, 2026-11-09: "the same dialogue as JC31, with MINOR (if any) switches… to allow for more visual interest"). He sent back both a full rebuild on the skeleton and a version with a new action hook. So the only changes are small wording switches that give the screen something to show, and each one is quoted in chat for a yes before it goes in. The skeleton above is for reading the winners and for a net-new song story, never for rewriting a proven script. No text hook unless Reed asks. The variations come from the music and the scenes, described in chat. Then run the scanner and paste a plain copy in a code block.
On RP3 he took a place detail that opens a wide shot ("way out at the end of the lot") and passed on a wardrobe detail repeated as a callback ("still in her knee pads"). So offer settings and space for the screen to show, not props pinned to the daughter.
